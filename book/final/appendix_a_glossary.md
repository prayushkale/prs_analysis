## Appendix A: Glossary

**ADR (American Depositary Receipt)** — a US-listed certificate representing shares of a foreign company; Indian companies with US listings produce overnight ADR signals for the Indian market the next morning.

**ATM/ITM/OTM** — at-the-money (strike near spot), in-the-money (strike already favourable), out-of-the-money (strike unfavourable).

**Bank Nifty** — index of India's largest banks; the most-traded index after Nifty for options.

**Block deal** — a single large trade (typically ≥₹10 crore or 500+ shares) executed off the order book between two parties.

**Breakeven (options)** — the underlying level at which a position stops losing: strike plus (for call buys) or minus (for put buys) the premium paid; for sold options, the same points mark where losses begin.

**Calendar spread** — selling an option in one expiry and buying the same-strike option in a farther expiry; profits from time-decay differentials.

**Call option** — right (not obligation) to buy at the strike; the seller of a call has the obligation, for premium.

**Cash market** — the equity market for immediate delivery, as opposed to derivatives.

**Collateral** — pledged securities/FDs/cash that fund derivatives margin without cash outlay; the margin system's building block.

**Covered call** — selling a call against stock held; caps upside, earns premium.

**Crash / correction / bear market** — working definitions used in this book: a correction is roughly a 10% fall from highs; a bear market roughly 20%; a crash is a violent, multi-percent single-session or multi-session event.

**DII** — domestic institutional investor (mutual funds, insurers, domestic institutions).

**Doji** — a candlestick with open and close nearly equal, signalling indecision.

**Drawdown** — peak-to-trough decline of capital or index.

**Expiry** — the date an option contract settles. Indian index options moved from weekly expiries per index to a rationalized single-weekly-per-exchange system after 2024–25 regulation.

**F&O** — futures and options.

**FII** — foreign institutional investor (also FPI, foreign portfolio investor).

**Firefighting (adjustment)** — restructuring a losing position (rolling, hedging, ratioing) to reduce further damage rather than exiting outright.

**Gap up/down** — opening above/below the prior close, usually on overnight news.

**GIFT Nifty** — the Nifty futures contract traded at GIFT City (successor to SGX Nifty, which served the same role before July 2023); trades nearly 21 hours and indicates India's opening direction.

**Greeks** — sensitivity measures: Delta (price move per index point), Gamma (rate of change of Delta), Theta (time decay per day), Vega (sensitivity to implied volatility), Rho (rate sensitivity).

**Implied volatility (IV)** — the volatility embedded in option prices; IV Rank/Percentile compare current IV to its own history.

**India VIX** — the volatility index computed from Nifty options; readings below ~15 are low-volatility regimes, above ~30 signal panic.

**ITM put calendar** — selling a near-month in-the-money put and buying the same strike in a farther month; a margin-efficient, directionally-biased income structure.

**Lot size** — the fixed number of units per derivative contract; Nifty's lot moved from 75 to larger values in the 2024–25 regulatory tightening.

**Margin (SPAN + exposure)** — the collateral required to carry derivatives positions; SPAN is the portfolio risk calculation, exposure an additional buffer; peak-margin rules require intraday sufficiency.

**Mark-to-market (MTM)** — daily settlement of futures gains/losses.

**Max open interest (max OI) strike** — the strike with the largest open interest; treated by traders as a crowd-consensus support/resistance zone to be respected or faded.

**MSCI rebalancing** — periodic index-weight changes that force passive funds to buy/sell constituents mechanically.

**Open interest (OI)** — total outstanding derivative contracts; rising OI with rising prices signals new longs, etc.

**Option chain** — the table of all strikes with premiums, OI, and volumes for an underlying.

**Payoff** — the profit diagram of a position at expiry.

**PCR (put-call ratio)** — volume or OI of puts divided by calls; a sentiment gauge best read with positioning context.

**Physical settlement** — post-2019 Indian rule requiring delivery of shares for in-the-money stock options at expiry.

**Premium** — the option's price, paid by buyer, received by seller.

**Put option** — right to sell at strike.

**Qualified institutional placement (QIP)** — a fast private share issuance to institutions.

**Ratio spread** — buying and selling unequal numbers of options (e.g., buy 1, sell 2), creating a credit or low-cost directional structure with a capped or naked tail.

**Read-through trade** — trading Indian stocks on overnight signals from related foreign-listed securities (ADR moves, foreign peers' results).

**Rolling (a position)** — closing a nearing-expiry option and opening a farther one, often at a different strike.

**Short covering** — buying back borrowed/sold positions, producing sharp upside moves.

**Short straddle/strangle** — selling both a call and put (same strike for straddle, different for strangle); profits from range-bound decay, suffers from big moves.

**Short covering rally** — a rapid rally driven by shorts closing, not new buying.

**STT (Securities Transaction Tax)** — the turnover tax on trades; raised repeatedly, most sharply on options.

**Stop-loss** — a pre-set exit order; this book argues mechanical stop-losses are often inferior to position sizing plus adjustment for option sellers, and shows both sides.

**Straddle/strangle buyer** — one who buys both call and put to bet on a big move either way.

**Synthetic future** — a long call plus short put at the same strike, replicating futures exposure.

**Theta decay** — the acceleration of option time-value loss near expiry.

**Tug of war** — the recurring FII-selling vs DII/retail-buying dynamic that defined Indian markets 2021–2025.

**V-shaped recovery** — a sharp fall followed by an equally sharp rebound, as in 2020.

**Volatility crush** — the collapse of IV after an event (budget, results) passes, hurting option buyers.

**Whipsaw** — a false breakout that reverses, trapping both sides.
