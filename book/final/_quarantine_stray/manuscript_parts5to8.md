# Part V: Trading the Events

## Chapter 19 — The Event Playbook

Binary events — budget, elections, Fed decisions, CPI prints — are the highest-probability environments for option sellers, because IV is elevated before the event and collapses after. The playbook:

- **Enter 7–10 days before the event.** IV is building, premiums are rising.
- **Sell premium, buy protection.** A calendar or ratio spread captures the IV collapse.
- **Do not hold through the event unless hedged.** The event itself is binary.

### Budget Day

The Indian budget (typically February 1) is the single most consistent event for elevated IV. The budget can be bullish, bearish, or neutral — but the IV collapse after the announcement is nearly guaranteed. "I tell my students," Dhanraj said, "do not try to predict the budget. Predict the IV collapse."

### Election Day

Indian election results (general and state) produce massive volatility. The June 2024 general election result day saw Nifty fall 9% intraday and then recover 2,000 points in a week. The IV in the week before was at 30+ — near-panic levels. "In 2014 and 2019, India VIX went above 30 before the election. In 2024, it happened on the result day itself. The panic came late, but it came."

### Central Bank Days

The RBI and Fed decisions move currencies, bonds, and equities. Fed decisions affect Indian markets primarily through the dollar-rupee. "When the Fed speaks, India listens. But the reaction is often in the bond market, and Indian equities follow with a lag."

## Chapter 20 — Budgets and Elections: Case Files 2019-2025

### 2019: The Sitharaman Candle

In July 2019, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's first budget included a surcharge on FPIs that effectively raised their tax burden. FIIs fled, markets crashed — the resulting candlestick was named the "Sitharaman Candle" (a large bearish candle). Within weeks, the surcharge was rolled back, but the damage was done. The lesson: **never hold a large directional position through an unknown budget.**

### September 2019: Corporate Tax Cut

In one day, the government slashed corporate tax from 30% to 22% — a positive black swan. Markets shot up. "The Nirmala Sitharaman Candle was a negative black swan; the corporate tax cut was a positive one," Dhanraj said. Both happened under the same finance minister, six months apart. **The market can react violently in either direction.**

### 2024 Election: The 9% Intraday Crash

June 4, 2024. Exit polls had predicted a landslide BJP victory. The actual result was a coalition government, forcing BJP to rely on allies. Nifty crashed more than 9% at the intraday low. FIIs and DIIs were both sellers — but retail bought the dip. Within a week, Nifty had recovered 2,000 points. The retail buyer was right; the institutions, for once, were wrong.

"That was the most expensive mistake the institutions made in 2024," Dhanraj observed. "They sold the crash, retail bought it. The retail people are proving they are smarter than the professionals, at least about events."

## Chapter 21 — Wars, Crashes and Black Swans

### War Impact: The Indian Pattern

Historically, India-Pakistan conflicts have a consistent market impact:

| Event | Nifty Fall | Recovery |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Kargil War (1999) | ~5-6% | Complete, within 3 months |
| Parliament Attack (2001) | ~4-5% | Complete |
| Mumbai Terror Attack (2008) | ~4-5% | Complete (markets had bigger issues) |
| Pulwama/Balakot (2019) | ~4-5% | Complete within weeks |
| Ukraine War (2022) | ~12% | Complete within 6 months |

"The average is 4-5% fall," Dhanraj said, reviewing the history. "Sometimes other factors coincide — 2008 had Lehman, Kargil had dot-com bubble. But the war itself does not trigger a crash. It triggers a selloff of about one to two days, and then the market recovers."

### COVID-19 (2020): The Nifty 7,500 Bottom

The pandemic was the exception. Nifty fell from ~12,300 to ~7,500 in a month (~40%), the fastest and deepest fall in Indian history. Then it rallied to 17,000 within two years. "The COVID crash was a genuine black swan — nobody predicted it, nobody was hedged for it. But the recovery was equally unprecedented."

### Positive Black Swans

Not every black swan is negative. The corporate tax cut (September 2019) was a positive black swan. A sudden policy announcement, favorable court ruling, or ceasefire agreement can produce the same violent move upward.

# Part VI: A Market Chronicle, 2018–2026

*This section is a concise timeline of key market events drawn from verified data. For each event, the original claim by Dhanraj is noted alongside the verified fact where they differ.*

## Chapter 22 — 2018–2020: Tariffs, a Crash, and a Miracle

**Oct 2018 — FII Record Selling.** FIIs sold ₹30,000 crore in one month — their heaviest selling since January 2008. Nifty fell from 11,000 to 10,000. The trigger: global trade tensions (US-China tariffs) and domestic uncertainty ahead of the 2019 general election. [^9]

**Nov 2018 — Crude Oil's 12-Day Fall.** Brent crude fell for 12 consecutive days, the longest streak in 30 years. From $86 to below $65. The 7.5% single-day drop on Nov 13 was one of the largest. (Verified: Brent Nov 2018 timeline.)

**Feb 2019 — Balakot Airstrike.** Indian fighter jets struck in Pakistani territory. Nifty opened gap-up and rallied. FIIs, who had been selling since Nov 2018 on fears of a hung parliament, reversed to buying.

**May 2019 — BJP Wins General Election.** Markets rallied. The 2014–19 bull run resumed, built on corporate tax cuts and RBI rate cuts.

**Mar 2020 — COVID Crash.** Nifty fell from ~12,300 to ~7,500 (~40%). India's strictest lockdown. FIIs pulled $12B. The VIX touched 85. It was the most extreme event in Indian market history.

**Nov 2020 — Vaccine Rally.** Pfizer's 90%-efficacy vaccine announcement triggered Nifty's all-time high. From the March low, Nifty had recovered its entire loss in 8 months — a V-shaped recovery.

## Chapter 23 — 2021–2022: Liquidity Boom and Inflation Bust

**2021 — Retail Boom.** Nifty rose from 14,000 to 18,000. Retail investors opened record demat accounts. FIIs sold ₹20,000+ crore in several months, but DIIs and retail absorbed it all.

**May 2022 — The 1,200-Point Crash.** From 17,400 (April 29) to 16,200 (May 9) — 1,200 points in 5-6 sessions. Trigger: Fed 50bp hike + hawkish tone + inflation data (CPI 8.3% vs expected 8.1%). (Corrected: CPI was 8.3% in April 2022, released May 11. The May 4 Fed hike was 50bp.)

**Jun 2022 — Swiss National Bank's First Hike in 15 Years.** The SNB raised rates. European and Dow futures collapsed 800–900 points in minutes. Nifty fell 500 points intraday.

## Chapter 24 — 2023–2024: Bull Run and Its First Crack

**Jul 2023 — VIX at 12-Year Low (11.5).** The lowest since 2011. Low volatility regime dangerous for naked short sellers.

**Jan 2024 — Adani-Hindenburg.** Hindenburg Research published a report on Adani Group on January 24, 2023 (not 2024, as Dhanraj sometimes misdated in later references). Adani Enterprises fell from ₹3,900 to ₹1,017 over weeks. The FPO was withdrawn.

**Jun 2024 — Election Day Crash.** Nifty fell 9% intraday on June 4. Exit polls were wrong. Retail bought; institutions sold.

**Oct 2024 — Record FII Outflows.** FIIs sold more than ₹1 lakh crore in 3 months. Nifty fell 8% from ATH (26,277). The structural bid from DIIs absorbed the selling.

## Chapter 25 — 2025–2026: Tariffs, Jane Street, and the New Regime

**2025 — Trump Tariffs.** China tariffs escalated to 20%; Europe threatened with 50% tariffs on autos. Market volatility returned.

**May 2025 — Indo-Pak Conflict.** Cross-border clashes; Nifty fell ~5% initially, recovered within weeks (consistent with historical patterns).

**Jul 2025 — Jane Street Penalty.** SEBI issued a ~₹5,000 crore notice to Jane Street for algorithmic trading violations on expiry days. The debate about market manipulation reached its peak.

**2026 — The 50% Cash Rule Bite.** With SEBI's tightened norms and the phased implementation of one weekly expiry per exchange, F&O volumes declined from their 2024 peak. SEBI reported the drop as expected; Dhanraj's view: "They wanted to reduce volumes, and they succeeded."

# Part VII: Predators & Protectors

## Chapter 26 — How Retail Gets Cheated

### The Opposite-Account P&L Scam

An influencer posts screenshots of a winning account. What they do not show: the equal-and-opposite account where they are short the same positions, netting zero. "A trader whose capital never compounds despite posting crores in profits is likely running paired accounts," Dhanraj explained. "I started the MTM screenshot trend in 2017-18. I stopped when I saw the fakes multiplying."

### The Teacher Trap

**Warning signs:** Unreasonable return promises (>50% monthly). Flashy lifestyle content (luxury cars, rented apartments, bodyguards). "My workshop attendee made a crore" testimonials — without capital disclosure. Charges ₹50,000+ for a 2-day workshop that promises systems, not process.

**Verification:** Check Ministry of Corporate Affairs filings (₹100 fee). If the trainer's company shows losses while the personal lifestyle is lavish, the source of income is probably the training, not trading.

### Broker Hidden Charges

Brokers charge brokerage, STT, stamp duty, transaction charges, SEBI turnover fees, exchange charges, GST — often 5-8 separate line items. "Read your contract note line by line," Dhanraj advised. "Many brokers were eating the peak margin penalty and blaming it on SEBI. Always verify with the exchange's own margin calculator."

## Chapter 27 — The Regulator's Dilemma

### SEBI's Tightening

From 2020 to 2025, SEBI progressively tightened the derivatives market:

| Year | Change | Impact |
|------|--------|--------|
| Sep 2020 | Peak margin framework (50% cash) | Killed margin leverage |
| 2021 | Weekly expiry introduced | Increased volumes, then manipulation |
| Sep 2024 | Committee proposes one expiry/exchange | Volumes would fall |
| Nov 2024 | Lot size 75 → 25 (Nifty) | Made options more accessible, accidentally |
| 2025 | One expiry per exchange phased in | Volumes declined from peak |
| 2025 | Jane Street action | Sent message to Algos |

### The Jane Street Case (July 2025)

SEBI alleged that Jane Street, a global quantitative trading firm, manipulated expiry-day option prices through aggressive algorithmic trades. Fine: ~₹5,000 crore. "Whatever happened, SEBI was correct to act," Dhanraj said. "But the bigger issue is that a hedger and a speculator are treated the same — both pay high margins. In the US, if you hold shares and sell a call, you pay zero margin. In India, you pay SPAN + exposure."

## Chapter 28 — Taxes: The Trader's Real Opponent

### F&O as Business Income

For most traders, F&O income is treated as **business income** (not capital gains or speculative income). Pros: can offset with business expenses (computer, internet, course fees, research subscriptions). Cons: requires a separate turnover computation, audit if turnover exceeds ₹10 crore (₹10L for presumptive taxation), and filing ITR-3 or ITR-4.

### STT: The 8× Hike

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on options increased approximately **8 times over 10 years** (2015-2024). On futures, the hike from the 2024 budget was 150% (0.1% → 0.25% by budget changes). "Each increase was justified as 'deterrent for speculative trading.' The effect was to make every trade more expensive for the honest trader."

### Crypto Taxation (Budget 2022)

India's 30% flat tax on crypto gains + 1% TDS per transaction (Section 194S). The 1% TDS on every transaction (buy and sell) mathematically destroys the intraday trader. Dhanraj: "If you trade crypto 365 days a year, you have paid 365% TDS before you make any profit. You must wait for the refund. This is not taxation — it is prohibition through paperwork."

# Part VIII: Wealth Beyond the Screen

## Chapter 29 — Investing vs. Trading

Trading is about capturing short-term price dislocations with defined risk. Investing is about owning a piece of a growing economy over decades. "Do not confuse the two," Dhanraj said. "When you have a trading loss, it is a business expense. When you have an investment loss, it is a mistake you should not have made."

### Value vs. Momentum

Value investing (buy cheap stocks) works over long horizons. Momentum investing (buy recent winners) works over 3-12 month horizons. Both work. The error is to switch between them at the worst time.

## Chapter 30 — The Hard Assets Debate

### Real Estate: The Yield Arithmetic

Indian residential real estate yields 2–3%. Loans cost 8–9%. That is negative carry *before* maintenance, property tax, and legal costs. "You are betting on capital appreciation alone," Dhanraj argued. "And if that capital appreciation is 15% per year for the next 10 years, you still net less than a simple index fund after transaction costs."

### Gold

Gold is insurance against systemic crisis — debasement, war, currency collapse. As an investment, its 30-year return lags equities. But as a diversification tool (5–10% of a portfolio), it reduces drawdown in equity crashes.

## Chapter 31 — Crypto as a Satellite

### Sizing

"Treat crypto as pocket money — 5–10% of your trading capital, at most." The high IV (volatility) means even 5% can dominate portfolio returns if it moves 50% in a month — which it will.

### The Wide-Breakeven Design

"BTC option strategies must have wide breakevens — 10-20% on each side. Because Bitcoin can move 10% overnight. Your short ITM straddle from yesterday is today's deep OTM disaster." Use calendars, butterflies, and ratio spreads — never sell naked crypto options without a compensating buy.
