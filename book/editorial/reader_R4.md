## Voice & style notes
- Rigid weekly template, always the same skeleton: sponsor greeting → last-week recap with numbers → coming-week event calendar (results, data, politics) → OI-derived range/levels → directional bias with hedged wording → closing disclaimer ("my personal opinion... gut feeling... no data to support") → sign-off "hope you enjoyed watching, thank you for watching".
- Numbers are everywhere but deliberately approximate: "I think", "roughly", "very approximately", and once literally "Who bothers about accuracy?" (2817). The teaching point: levels are *areas of confluence* (Fibonacci + OI + straddle premium), not precise figures.
- Signature aphorisms recur across years: "markets react only to surprise news"; "markets do not react for the same bad news twice"; "amateurs open the market, professionals close the market"; "every dip is a buying opportunity"; "the trend is our friend until it bends"; "in a bull market only investors make money, traders do not"; "uncertainty is an uncertainty until it becomes certainty"; "hope is the worst enemy".
- Teaches by *case law*: 2004/2009/2014/2019 election surprises, Brexit exit polls, 2018 vs 2023 state elections, Lehman day, Covid day — each doctrine is anchored to a dated story.
- Personal trades shown as worked examples with honest P&L (rolled 17,000P→18,000P; ₹8L profit vs ₹13L charges; friends' 5→150 put squeeze) — great raw material for the Strategy Library if genericized.
- Self-aware humor: "there is no stock in the name of Sunder, otherwise I cannot even say hi" (2333, SEBI naming rule).
- Repetition is a feature: the same doctrine (surprise rule, FII/DII tug-of-war) is restated with fresh evidence each week — the book should state each doctrine once with its best 2–3 case studies.
- Audio-only episodes (workshop days) still carry full analysis — format never dictates content.
- Bias is always stated with an escape hatch: "I may be wrong", "probability is a different game" — model this epistemic honesty in the book's voice.
