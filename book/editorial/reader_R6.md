## Voice & style notes
- Fixed skeleton: name-intro → "special episode" framing → hook (news or audience question) → first-principles build-up with a household analogy → on-screen data/backtest → numbers-heavy proof (ROI, margin, breakevens, annualization) → disclaimers → workshop CTA → "Hope you enjoyed watching this video. Thank you for watching."
- Analogies are his signature: insurance/salaried family (hedging), waiting for the bus (corrections), dog chase (adjustments), aeroplane company (capital gating), arranged-marriage search (strategy fallibility), small fish → big fish → whales (order flow).
- Converts monthly returns to annualized and benchmarks against business returns ("99% of businessmen don't make 43% p.a.") — persuasive scaffolding worth preserving.
- Spoken redundancy: core rules repeated 2–3× per video; the book should state each once, crisply.
- Anonymous-character storytelling (friend, villager, cousin, taxi driver) to deliver risk lessons without naming anyone.
- Self-deprecating humility plus troll deflection ("my view can always go wrong, I am not the god").
- Opinionated regulator/media framing ("the media decides the budget before the finance minister does").
- Risk philosophy is consistent: define risk first, size small, keep hedges real (not margin-dummies), verify before trusting.
