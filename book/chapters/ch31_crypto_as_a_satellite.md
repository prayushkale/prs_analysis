# Chapter 31: Crypto as a Satellite

## Wealth Beyond the Screen

---

On a humid Tuesday evening in August 2021, a twenty-six-year-old software engineer from Pune sat staring at a screen that showed his account balance had just crossed $7,500. Seven weeks earlier, he had transferred ₹1.5 lakh into a crypto exchange account — money he had saved by skipping his annual vacation for two years. His friends called him reckless. His father called him a gambler. But the screen showed a 350% return, and to him, that was proof.

What the screen did not show was the path. It did not show the night in late June when his position dropped 42% in six hours because a Chinese regulatory tweet sent Bitcoin spiraling. It did not show the three sleepless nights he spent watching his margin call loom closer. It did not show that he was using options strategies he barely understood on an exchange with no customer support phone number.

I met him three months later, after a market crash had erased most of his gains. He asked me a question I have heard hundreds of times since: "Sir, should I exit crypto entirely?"

I told him the truth: neither his original all-in bet nor a complete exit was investing. Both were reactions. The first was greed; the second was fear. What he needed was a framework.

This chapter is that framework.

## The Core and the Satellite

Imagine you are building a house in a flood-prone area. You need a structure that can withstand water — strong pillars, a raised foundation, solid walls. That is your core portfolio: index funds, quality stocks, fixed income, perhaps real estate. These are the assets whose long-term behaviour you can study, whose cash flows you can analyse, whose management you can evaluate. Over decades, they have shown they can weather storms and still grow.

Now imagine you also keep a small boat in your backyard. You do not live in it. You do not store your savings in it. But when the flood comes — and it will come — the boat might let you reach higher ground. And if no flood comes, the boat is a small cost to maintain for the peace of mind it brings.

That boat is crypto.

Or think of it differently. In astronomy, a satellite is an object that orbits something larger. It is not the centre of the system. It does not provide the gravitational pull that keeps everything together. But it has its own orbit, its own momentum, and it can be useful — so long as it remembers what it is orbiting around.

The phrase I want you to remember from this chapter is this: **Crypto is a satellite, not the sun.**

What does this mean in practice? It means your crypto allocation should be small enough that if it went to zero tomorrow, your life plans would not change. It should be small enough that you never feel compelled to check its price every hour. It should be small enough that the emotional noise it generates does not bleed into your decisions about your core portfolio.

In the Indian context, I typically suggest that most investors keep their total crypto exposure — across coins, tokens, options, and any crypto-adjacent instruments — at 2% to 5% of their total investable wealth. For a young professional with high risk tolerance and no dependents, 5% is acceptable. For someone near retirement, 1% to 2% is wiser. Rather than a fixed formula, treat this as a ceiling: the discipline is never exceeding the cap, even when crypto is flying.

Why so small? Because crypto's history is a history of violent, sudden, and inexplicable moves. In March 2020, Bitcoin fell nearly 50% in two days. In May 2021, it fell from $58,000 to $30,000 in three weeks. In November 2022, after the FTX collapse, the entire market shed over $200 billion in days. Even in the celebrated 2024 bull run, a single regulatory announcement could shave 10% off prices in an afternoon. Experienced traders make money in these swings, but only with money they can afford to lose — capital whose loss does not change their standard of living.

This is not pessimism. It is arithmetic. If you put 50% of your wealth into crypto and crypto falls 80%, you have lost 40% of your total wealth — a catastrophe that takes years to recover from. If you put 5% of your wealth into crypto and crypto falls 80%, you have lost 4% — a bad quarter in the stock market. The difference between 40% and 4% is not arithmetic; it is psychology. One keeps you awake at night; the other lets you sleep, wait, and eventually act with a clear head when prices recover.

## How the Crypto Machine Actually Works

Before discussing strategies, let us understand what we are dealing with. Crypto assets are digital tokens secured by blockchain technology — a distributed ledger that records transactions across many computers. Unlike shares of an Indian company, crypto does not represent ownership in a business with earnings, assets, or management. There is no P/E ratio, no book value, no dividend yield. The value of a crypto asset comes from what market participants believe others will pay for it in the future.

This makes crypto closer to currency trading than equity investing. And like currencies, crypto prices are driven by three forces: liquidity, narrative, and regulation.

**Liquidity** is the availability of money to buy. When global central banks pump money into the economy, some of it finds its way into crypto. When money tightens, crypto tends to fall because it is among the first assets investors sell.

**Narrative** is the story that justifies the price. In 2017, the story was "blockchain will revolutionise everything." In 2021, it was "crypto is digital gold and an inflation hedge." In 2024, it was "Bitcoin ETFs will bring institutional money." Each narrative seemed persuasive at the time. Each was later questioned.

**Regulation** is the government's stance. In India, crypto exists in a strange legal grey zone. You can trade it, but it is not legal tender. The government does not ban it, but it taxes it heavily: a flat 30% tax on gains with no deduction for losses, plus a 1% TDS on every trade. This tax regime — unusual by international standards — creates a powerful incentive for long-term holding and a powerful disincentive for frequent trading.

When you understand these three forces, you understand why crypto behaves the way it does. Prices do not move because companies report earnings. They move because a whale (a large holder) sells or buys, because a central bank signals a rate move, because a regulator hints at a ban, because a celebrity tweets, because a story catches fire on social media. These are not investment factors; they are trading factors.

This is why the strategies that work in the stock market do not transfer neatly to crypto. You cannot "buy and hold" in the same way, because there is no underlying earnings growth to carry you through a bear market. You cannot "average down" with conviction, because you have no valuation anchor to tell you when the asset is cheap. You can only do one thing reliably: size your position so that you survive the chaos, and use the market's volatility to your advantage when it presents clear opportunities.

## The Premium-Selling Dampener

Now I want to share a technique that has been used by several of my students — the one approach to crypto active trading that has shown consistent results in my recorded discussions. It is not a get-rich-quick scheme. It is a way to generate income from crypto's extreme volatility without betting on price direction.

It is called **premium selling**, and it works like this.

Crypto exchanges offer options — contracts that give you the right to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price before a certain date. Because crypto is so volatile, the premiums (the prices of these options) are extraordinarily high. A far out-of-the-money Bitcoin put option that might cost you ₹50 in the index market could cost $500 or more in crypto. The premium is high because the risk is high. But if you are selling that option — meaning you are collecting the premium and taking on the obligation — the high price is your friend, provided you manage the risk.

Here is a worked example, based on an actual case I discussed with a Delhi-based trader in 2023.

The trader, whom I will call Amit, had ₹8 lakh in a crypto exchange account. That was his entire speculative capital — money he was willing to lose. He did not, however, want to lose it. His goal was to generate 3% to 5% per month in income by selling put options on Bitcoin and Ethereum.

The strategy worked like this: On the first of each month, Amit would sell put options on Bitcoin with a strike price 10-15% below the current spot price, expiring at the end of the month. If Bitcoin stayed above the strike price, the options would expire worthless, and he kept the entire premium — typically 2-4% of the notional value. If Bitcoin fell below the strike price, he would be obligated to buy Bitcoin at that price. But because the strike was 10-15% below market, he would be buying on a significant discount.

In the first month, Bitcoin was trading around $28,000. Amit sold puts with a strike of $24,000, collecting a premium of approximately $300 per contract. Each contract obligated him to buy one Bitcoin at $24,000 if it fell that far. He had to hold margin in his account — roughly $2,400 per contract — which meant he could sell about three contracts with his capital.

Bitcoin ended the month at $29,500. The options expired worthless. Amit kept $900 in premium on a margin of about $7,200 — a 12% monthly return on margin, or roughly 3% on his total capital.

He repeated this for six months. In five of those six months, Bitcoin stayed above his strike prices. He collected premiums totaling approximately $5,000 on his $8,000 account. In the one bad month, Bitcoin crashed 20% in a week, and he was assigned Bitcoin at his strike price. He now held Bitcoin that was immediately worth 5-8% less than what he paid.

But — and this is the critical part — he had collected $5,000 in premiums over the previous months. His total profit was still in positive territory. His loss on the Bitcoin position was smaller than his accumulated income. He could either hold the Bitcoin, sell covered calls against it, or simply wait for a bounce.

Let me give you the complete table of his account evolution:

| Month | Bitcoin (start) | Strike Sold | Premium Collected | Outcome |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Month 1 | $28,000 | $24,000 (Put) | $900 | Expired worthless |
| Month 2 | $29,500 | $25,000 (Put) | $800 | Expired worthless |
| Month 3 | $31,200 | $26,500 (Put) | $750 | Expired worthless |
| Month 4 | $30,500 | $25,500 (Put) | $850 | Expired worthless |
| Month 5 | $26,000 | $22,000 (Put) | $1,100 | Expired worthless |
| Month 6 | $32,000 | $27,000 (Put) | $700 | Assigned at $27,000; spot $24,800 |
| **Total** | — | — | **$5,100** | **Net positive despite assignment** |

Notice something important. His profit did not come from being right about Bitcoin's direction. It came from being patient about volatility. He did not predict crashes; he priced them. He used high premiums to his advantage, effectively charging rent on his capital while waiting for a good entry price.

Amit's experience is not a promise that this strategy always works. There was one student, I recall from a session, who used the same approach on an unregulated exchange — a platform not registered in India, with no customer protection. The exchange went offline for three days during a volatile weekend, and he could not adjust his positions. When it came back, his margin had been liquidated at unfavourable prices. He lost ₹3.5 lakh, more than his accumulated income.

The lesson is not about the strategy itself. It is about the infrastructure. In crypto, the exchange is your counterparty. If it fails — through hacking, mismanagement, or outright fraud — your positions and your money can vanish. The FTX collapse of 2022 should have made this clear to everyone. That is why I insist on using only exchanges that are registered or operating compliant with Indian regulations, like Delta Exchange, which offers crypto options in Indian rupees with proper know-your-customer norms. Even then, I keep the majority of my settlement assets in a separate cold wallet, not on the exchange.

## The Three Questions Before You Start

Before you allocate any money to crypto, I want you to answer three questions honestly.

**First: Can I afford to lose 100% of this amount without changing my life?** This is the "sleep test." If the answer is no, your allocation is too big. Whatever your monthly expenses, whatever your savings goals, whatever your family obligations — they must be fully settled before crypto gets even one rupee.

**Second: Am I prepared to not check the price for thirty days?** If the thought of not checking fills you with anxiety, you are in the wrong asset class. Crypto is not designed for anxious people. It is designed for people who can let go of short-term outcomes and focus on long-term probabilities.

**Third: Do I understand the specific instrument I am using?** There is a world of difference between buying Bitcoin on a regulated exchange and trading leveraged futures on an unregistered platform. Each has different risks, different tax treatments, different regulatory protections. If you cannot explain the instrument to a friend in two minutes, you do not understand it well enough to put money in it.

These three questions, asked honestly, will eliminate most of the people who should never touch crypto. The remaining few — the ones who can afford to lose the money, who can detach emotionally, and who understand what they are actually doing — those are the ones who can treat crypto as a satellite.

## The Satellite in an Indian Portfolio

Let me now put this in the Indian context, because the numbers change everything.

Suppose you have ₹10 lakh of investable wealth. You are thirty years old, earning ₹15 lakh per year, renting an apartment, with no dependents. You decide to allocate 5% — ₹50,000 — to crypto.

How should that ₹50,000 be distributed?

In my experience, a sensible structure looks like this:

The core of your satellite is Bitcoin and Ethereum. These are the most established, most liquid, most institutionally recognised crypto assets. They are not safe — nothing in crypto is safe — but they are less likely to go to zero than smaller altcoins. Put 60% of your crypto allocation here.

A second ring consists of established altcoins — assets like Solana, Polygon, or similar that have real development teams, active communities, and some track record. These have higher upside but higher risk. Put 25% here.

The final ring is for speculative trades, including crypto options or newer tokens. This is where you might use some of the premium-selling strategies from earlier, or where you might make small directional bets. Put 15% here — and accept that you will probably lose some of it.

Let me work through the numbers. With your ₹50,000 crypto allocation, you put ₹30,000 in Bitcoin/Ethereum, ₹12,500 in altcoins, and ₹7,500 in speculative trades. If the entire speculative portion goes to zero — which happens more often than you would think — you lose ₹7,500. That is 0.75% of your total wealth. The rest of your portfolio — your index funds, your stocks, your emergency fund — is untouched.

Now imagine that a strong crypto bull market happens, as it did in 2024. Your Bitcoin and Ethereum double, your altcoins triple, and your speculative trades return 50%. Your ₹50,000 becomes approximately ₹1.1 lakh — a gain of ₹60,000. That is a 12% boost to your total portfolio, not from speculation but from a disciplined satellite strategy.

And if the market crashes instead? Your Bitcoin and Ethereum fall 60%, your altcoins fall 70%, and your speculation goes to zero. Your ₹50,000 becomes roughly ₹14,500. You have lost ₹35,500 — about 3.5% of your total wealth. Painful, but survivable. You do not sell anything. You wait. You collect premiums with whatever remains. And over time, you recover.

The key insight is that the satellite does not need to grow fast. It needs only two things: to be small enough to be survivable when wrong, and to have asymmetric upside when right.

## The Story the Screen Does Not Show

Let me return to the young engineer from Pune. After his losses, he asked me whether he should sell everything and never look at crypto again. I asked him how much he had lost, as a percentage of his total savings.

He was silent for a moment. "About 12%," he said.

"Then your problem was not crypto," I told him. "Your problem was concentration. You took a speculative asset and gave it more weight than it deserved. The asset behaved exactly as it always had — volatile, unpredictable, violent. You were the one who mismatched the risk. Crypto did not break the rules; you broke them."

He did not put all his money into crypto again. But six months later, he started a small, systematic position: 3% of his portfolio, using a premium-selling approach on a regulated Indian exchange. His returns have been modest — 1.5% to 2% per month — but consistent. More importantly, he has stopped checking prices every hour. He has time for his work, his health, his relationships. The screen no longer controls his life.

That, ultimately, is what "Wealth Beyond the Screen" means. The screen is a tool, not a master. Crypto is a satellite, not the sun. When you understand these truths, you can participate in the crypto economy without being consumed by it.

You can have your boat in the backyard, ready for the flood. But you still live in the house.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat crypto as a satellite allocation, not as your core portfolio.** Keep it at 2-5% of investable wealth, and never exceed the cap even during euphoric bull markets. The discipline of the ceiling matters more than picking the right coin.

- **Understand what moves crypto prices.** Liquidity, narrative, and regulation drive this market, not company earnings or valuation fundamentals. Strategies that work in stocks do not automatically work here.

- **Premium selling — owning options, not chasing direction — is the most sustainable way to be active in crypto.** Selling put options collects rent from volatility and only obligates you when prices fall to levels you want to buy anyway.

- **Use only regulated exchanges and keep settlement assets in your own wallet.** Crypto's biggest risk is often not the price, but the platform you are using. The FTX lesson was expensive for a reason.
