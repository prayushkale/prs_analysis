# Chapter 26: How Retail Gets Cheated

## Core Lessons

1. *"A manager trading his own account with near-zero negotiated costs can show 70%+ annual returns that are structurally impossible for clients paying retail costs."*

2. *"A claimant who won't show capital, ROI, or live back-office P&L is selling greed, not skill."*

3. *"If brokers offered read-only logins, anyone could audit a trader's real P&L and the scam would die."*

4. *"Simplicity beats sophistication: complex jargon is often a marketing weapon, not a competence signal."*

5. *"If easy money exists, the broker takes it, the government takes it, and the manager takes the rest - the client gets peanuts."*

6. *"Tips services add a second loss layer — advisors face no audited track-record requirement before selling calls."*

7. *"There is no holy-grail strategy - anyone selling guaranteed strategies is running a fraud."*

8. *"For property exposure, liquid REITs beat direct ownership for most investors."*

9. *"If a strategy cannot be explained simply, distrust the seller."*

10. *"After 30–42% tax, promised pre-tax returns become impossible; check the post-tax number."*

11. *"F&O is a negative-sum game: winners' gains are smaller than losers' losses by the ₹50-60k crore/year cost drain."*

12. *"Pledging should cost the CDSL rate (about ₹5-10), never a percentage of value."*


## Strategy Spotlight

### Cost-audit of a typical intraday iron-fly money-manager operation

**Construction:** Sell ATM straddle + buy 1,000-1,500 point wings (Bank Nifty ~39,100); ~60,000 margin per lot set; 15 lot sets on 10 lakh capital; squared off daily

**Numbers:** 120 lots/day x 12 rupees brokerage = ~1,800/day = 4.5 lakh/year; STT+stamp ~3 lakh/year; slippage ~2.5 lakh/year; total ~10 lakh = entire capital; manager's own equivalent costs ~1,000/month

**Best used when:** Audit template for anyone evaluating a money manager

**Adjustments:** N/A

**Risks:** Client returns are negative before strategy risk even begins


### Read-only broker access (proposed fix)

**Construction:** Brokers issue a second read-only username/password for the back office; claimants share it publicly so anyone can inspect realized P&L and MTM

**Numbers:** n/a

**Best used when:** Industry-wide: would eliminate fake-screenshot and paired-account fraud

**Adjustments:** n/a

**Risks:** Requires broker implementation; privacy concerns for those with multiple businesses


### REIT替代 direct real estate

**Construction:** Allocate to listed REITs/InvITs instead of physical property

**Numbers:** India rental yield ~2-3%; home loans ~8-9%; Dubai property loan cited at 4.3% with 6-7% yields

**Best used when:** any investor seeking property exposure

**Adjustments:** n/a

**Risks:** listed REIT price volatility; underlying vacancy


### Investor patience framework at highs

**Construction:** Investors: hold cash ladder, buy only on 10-20-30% drawdown rungs or crisis events; prefer large caps over mid/small caps at highs. Traders: momentum allowed with technical stops

**Numbers:** Nifty PE cited ~30-35 vs ~10 typical for EMs; rollover ~170 pts/month

**Best used when:** Post-melt-up, pre-event (election/budget) windows

**Adjustments:** None - pre-committed patience rules

**Risks:** Opportunity cost if the melt-up continues; ladder never triggers in a one-way market


## Key Takeaways

- A manager trading his own account with near-zero negotiated costs can show 70%+ annual returns that are structurally impossible for clients paying retail costs.

- Simplicity beats sophistication: complex jargon is often a marketing weapon, not a competence signal.
