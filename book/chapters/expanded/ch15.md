# Chapter 15: Time Is the Trade: Calendar Spreads

## Core Lessons

1. *"Rolling the short put weekly across a consolidating month compounds small wins: ~25% on margin in three weeks in the worked example."*

2. *"Aggregate earnings statistics (share of companies beating, sales/profit growth) gauge whether a rally has fundamental breadth."*

3. *"Momentum: entry AND exit matter; value: only entry matters - know which game you are playing."*

4. *"Know your margin with and without the financing legs: 2 lakh with the strangle vs 70K without changes both risk sizing and return percentages."*

5. *"Same failure mode as all calendars: a strong directional move or an IV collapse hurts; the structure is for consolidation regimes."*

6. *"Put calendar mirrors the call calendar: long far-dated put, short weekly puts - harvest decay while keeping tail protection."*

7. *"Enter hedged structures only on net credit: premium received must exceed protection paid - never pay out of pocket to open."*

8. *"Results timing is tradable information: premium sellers position minutes before announcements to capture the IV crush."*

9. *"Calendars are net-debit structures; finance the debit by selling an adjacent strangle only if you also accept the larger margin that comes with it."*

10. *"Never start SIPs at all-time highs; stagger: invest 1/3 at -10%, 2/3 at -20%, all at -30%."*

11. *"Buying protection in a far month instead of near OTM wings slashes hedge bleed while capping disaster risk."*

12. *"Every 2-3 years a crisis gives value investors their entry: 2008, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2022."*


## Strategy Spotlight

### Deep-ITM short straddle with far-month protective put

**Construction:** Sell ATM+1 deep-ITM straddle near month (Sept 17,000 CE+PE with spot ~16,500); buy same-strike put in far month (Dec 17,000 PE)

**Numbers:** Entry ~45 days to expiry; Sept combined premium 689 vs Dec put 678 (net credit ~11); margin ~Rs 1 lakh/lot; 1-month backtest 1,174 to 572 = +72/share; ~3.6%/month or 66% annualized on actual trade

**Best used when:** Mildly bullish consolidation with elevated far-month IV; also works as directional view with disaster cap

**Adjustments:** Roll/adjust the short legs as market moves; may add capital; far put stays as backstop

**Risks:** Locks modest max profit; large fast moves cause MTM pain before adjustment; far-month put loses value if market drifts up slowly


### Staggered crash-buying ladder (investor)

**Construction:** At Nifty -10% from high: deploy 1/3 of earmarked capital; at -20%: another 1/3; at -30%: remainder

**Numbers:** Since 2008 only Lehman (2008) and COVID (2020) produced >30% drawdowns; typical corrections 10-20%

**Best used when:** High-valuation markets; patient long-term capital

**Adjustments:** None - it is a pre-committed ladder, not a forecast

**Risks:** Requires patience and cash discipline; drawdown can overshoot the last rung


### Put calendar (weekly rolling, debit-financed)

**Construction:** Buy 25,000 put in July series; sell 25,000 put in successive weekly expiries (June 12, 19, 26, then July weeks); financed by selling 24,000PE and 26,000CE (~Rs 350 collected) to offset the ~Rs 239 net debit

**Numbers:** Bought July 25,000PE ~Rs 558; sold weekly at ~319 (closed ~112), ~249 (closed ~206); margin ~Rs 2 lakh with strangle, ~Rs 70K without; paper P&L ~Rs 30,000 (~15%) or ~Rs 17,000 (~25% ex-strangle) in ~

**Best used when:** Post-shock consolidation with mild downward drift or range trade around the long strike

**Adjustments:** Close and re-sell the short put weekly; if range view fails, unwind the short strangle first to cut margin; roll the long put outward if the consolidation extends

**Risks:** Sharp fall through the long strike late in the trade, or IV crush after event weeks; strangle legs add naked risk and margin
