# Chapter 20: Budgets and Elections: Case Files

## The 9:55 AM Surprise

On September 20, 2019, I was sitting in my study, watching the Nifty drift lower. It was a Friday. The market had been weak all week. Rumours were circulating about a possible corporate tax cut, but nobody believed the government would actually do it. The fiscal deficit was already stretched. The GDP growth was slowing. Every analyst on television said the same thing: “Expectations are low, so any positive surprise will be good, but don’t hold your breath.”

At 9:55 AM, the Finance Minister stood up in Parliament and announced that the corporate tax rate for domestic companies would be slashed from 30% to 22%. Effective immediately. No grandfathering. No conditions. Just a straight cut.

Within minutes, the Nifty shot up nearly 4%. By the end of the day, it had gained almost 8% — one of the biggest single-day rallies in Indian history. Traders who had bought call options the night before saw their premiums turn into 10x, 20x, even 50x returns. Those who were short — or worse, short on futures — were wiped out.

That day taught me a lesson I have never forgotten: **In event-driven markets, the only thing that matters is the gap between expectation and reality.**

This chapter is about two kinds of high-impact events — budgets and elections. They are different in nature, but they share one critical feature: they are **known-date events**. You know exactly when the announcement will happen. That makes them tradable. Unlike a war or a pandemic, you can plan for a budget or an election result. You can size your bets, hedge your risks, and walk away with a profit — if you understand the mechanics.

We will walk through real case files from Indian markets. No theory. Only what worked, what failed, and what you can apply next time.

## First Principles: The Certainty of Uncertainty

Every trader knows that markets hate uncertainty. But not all uncertainty is the same.

There is **dated uncertainty** — the kind where the outcome is unknown but the date is fixed. Budget day, election result day, RBI policy day. These are events where the market has had weeks or months to form an expectation. The actual announcement either confirms that expectation — in which case the market barely moves — or surprises it, causing a violent repricing.

Then there is **open-ended uncertainty** — the Russia-Ukraine war, a sudden lockdown, a banking crisis. You do not know when it will start or when it will end. These events are dangerous because you cannot plan for them. As a trader, your first rule should be: *Stand aside from open-ended uncertainty. Trade only what has a date.*

Budgets and elections are the purest form of dated uncertainty. They happen at a fixed time. You can prepare. You can position. And you can exit before the next unknown storm.

Think of it like a cricket match. You know the date, the time, and the venue. You do not know who will win, but you can study the pitch, the weather, the players’ form. You can bet on the toss or on the first innings score. That is a tradable event. A sudden earthquake during the match? That is not.

## Case File 1: The Budget Leak — Testing the Waters

Let me tell you about a technique the Indian Finance Ministry has used for years: **the source-based leak**.

A few days before the Union Budget, a journalist will publish a story quoting “government sources” about a potential tax change or spending increase. The market reacts. If the reaction is too negative — say, a proposed tax on capital gains causes a 3% fall — the government quietly backs off. A new leak appears, suggesting the proposal was only “under consideration” and is now shelved.

If the reaction is positive, the measure stays in the budget.

Why does this matter to you? Because it means the budget itself is often anticlimactic. By the time the Finance Minister stands up, half the surprises have already been tested on the market. The real volatility happens in the weeks *before* the budget, during the leak season.

In February 2024, for example, the GDP growth estimate was leaked at 6.4% — a sharp drop from the previous 8.2%. The market fell. The Finance Ministry quickly countered with a leak suggesting that the budget would focus on infrastructure spending, calming the nerves. Traders who understood this game — and bought the dip on the leak — profited when the market recovered after the budget.

**The lesson**: Do not wait for budget day. Watch the leaks. They are signals, not noise. If a leak causes a sharp move, ask yourself: Is the government testing the water? If the move seems overdone, consider taking a contrarian position — but only if the date is still a few days away.

## Case File 2: The 2024 Election Shock — The 8.5% Intraday Fall

June 4, 2024. Lok Sabha counting day.

Every exit poll had predicted a landslide for the BJP-led NDA. The market had priced in a comfortable majority. Nifty was near 23,000. Options premiums were sky-high, but most traders were buying calls or selling puts — expecting a gap-up.

At 8:00 AM, the first trends showed the NDA leading in fewer seats than expected. By 10:00 AM, it was clear: the BJP itself might fall short of 272. The market panicked.

Nifty opened at 22,800 and fell like a rock. By 12:30 PM, it hit a low of 21,281 — a drop of nearly 8.5% from the previous close. It was the worst intraday crash since March 2020.

Traders who had bought put options on the morning of June 3 — paying a premium of ₹50-60 for a 22,500 put — saw that option trade at ₹1,200 by noon. A 20x return in four hours.

Those who were long futures — or worse, had sold puts — were margin-called before lunch.

But here is where the story gets interesting. By evening, the TDP and JDU had pledged support. The NDA would form the government. The next day, Nifty opened with a 3% gap-up. Over the next three months, the index recovered all its losses and went on to hit record highs — 32 new all-time highs before September ended.

The market had overreacted. The shock was real, but the long-term impact was zero.

**What should a disciplined trader have done?**

### Worked Example: Pre-Election Straddle vs. Post-Event Futures

Let me walk you through two approaches using real numbers from June 2024.

**Scenario**: On June 3, 2024, Nifty closed at 23,000. You have ₹10 lakh capital.

**Approach A — Pre-election straddle**:
- Buy one lot (50 units) of 23,000 call option at ₹200 premium (cost = ₹10,000).
- Buy one lot (50 units) of 23,000 put option at ₹180 premium (cost = ₹9,000).
- Total cost: ₹19,000.

On June 4, Nifty falls to 21,281 at one point. The 23,000 put option is trading at ₹1,200. You sell it at ₹1,200 (profit = ₹1,200 - ₹180 = ₹1,020 per unit = ₹51,000). The call option expires worthless.

Net profit = ₹51,000 - ₹9,000 (put premium) - ₹10,000 (call premium lost) = ₹32,000 on a capital of ₹10 lakh — a 3.2% return in one day.

But wait: if the result had been a clean sweep, the put would have expired worthless and the call would have rallied. The straddle would still have profited — just from the other side. That is the beauty of a straddle: it profits from *volatility*, not direction.

**Approach B — Post-event futures**:
You wait until the market opens on June 4. You see the panic. You short Nifty futures at 22,500. But by the time you execute, the market is already down 2%. You hold until the low of 21,281, then cover. Profit: 1,219 points × 50 units = ₹60,950.

Sounds better, right? But here is the problem: you have no idea if the market will bounce or keep falling. You are trading on emotion. Most traders who shorted on June 4 morning covered their shorts at the bottom out of fear, and then went long — only to see the market fall another 3%. They lost money both ways.

The straddle removes the emotional guesswork. You are betting on a big move, not a direction.

**Key takeaway**: For known-date events, use options to buy volatility. Never carry leveraged futures positions across the gap.

## Case File 3: The Corporate Tax Cut — The One-Stroke Policy

Let me return to that September 2019 day.

The government was under pressure. The economy was slowing. The FPI surcharge imposed earlier that year had caused foreign investors to flee — the Nifty had fallen 10% in two months. Then, on August 23, the Finance Minister announced that the surcharge would be rolled back for FPIs. The market rallied 3%.

But the real bomb came on September 20.

The corporate tax cut was not just a routine policy change. It was a *shock therapy* — a single stroke that reduced the effective tax rate for most companies from 35% to 25%. The government also abolished the Minimum Alternate Tax for companies that opted for the new regime.

The Nifty jumped from 10,800 to 11,800 in two sessions. That is a 9.3% rally. Every futures trader who was long made a fortune. Every put seller lost.

How could you have traded this?

You could not have predicted the exact day. But you could have observed the pattern: the government had already made a U-turn on the FPI surcharge. It was clear they were desperate to revive the economy. The probability of a big fiscal stimulus was rising.

**The technique**: Use a **call spread funded by FD interest**.

Here is how it works:

Suppose you have ₹10 lakh in a fixed deposit earning 8% per annum. That gives you ₹80,000 interest per year, or about ₹6,666 per month. Instead of withdrawing that interest, use it to buy an out-of-the-money call spread before a known event.

For example, before the 2019 corporate tax announcement, the Nifty was at 10,800. You could have bought a 11,000 call and sold a 11,500 call — a spread costing about ₹60 per lot. With ₹6,666, you could buy 111 spreads (each spread 50 units = ₹3,000 cost per spread? Actually let's calculate: ₹60 premium per spread (50 units) = ₹3,000 per spread. With ₹6,666 you can buy 2 spreads. Not much. Better to use a ratio: 1 spread per ₹3,000, you can buy 2 spreads for ₹6,000. If Nifty rallies to 11,500, the spread is worth 500 points × 50 = ₹25,000 per spread. Two spreads = ₹50,000 profit. That is a 7.5x return on your interest capital.

If the trade goes wrong, you lose only the interest — not your FD principal.

This technique is low risk, low commitment. You are using the bank’s money (your interest) to gamble on a known-date event. Over time, even if you lose 3 out of 4 such bets, the one winner will cover all losses and give you a net profit.

## The Framework: How to Trade Any Budget or Election

From these case files, you can extract a repeatable process:

1. **Identify the date** — Mark it on your calendar. Two weeks before, start watching the news for leaks and whispers.

2. **Assess the consensus** — What is the market expecting? If everyone expects a landslide win or a tax cut, the surprise will be on the downside. If expectations are low, any positive news will cause a rally.

3. **Position using options, not futures** — Use straddles or strangles for binary events. Use call/put spreads for directional bets. Never take a naked futures position across the event — the gap can destroy you.

4. **Size based on your risk capital** — Do not bet your life savings on a budget day. Use a small portion of your portfolio, or better, use the interest from your fixed deposits.

5. **Exit before the close** — Many traders make the mistake of holding overnight after a budget or election. The next day often has a gap in the opposite direction. Take your profit — or loss — by 3:30 PM.

6. **Ignore the noise** — Do not watch television during the event. The anchors will scream and panic. Stick to your plan. The market will always revert to the mean within a few days — unless the event fundamentally changes the economy.

## A Word on Risk

I have seen traders blow up their accounts on budget day more than on any other single day. The reason is always the same: they bet too big, or they bet without a plan.

One trader I knew — let us call him R — borrowed money to buy deep out-of-the-money call options before the 2024 budget. He was convinced the government would announce a massive stimulus. When the budget was lacklustre, the options expired worthless. He lost ₹12 lakh — his entire year’s savings, plus borrowed money.

Do not be R.

The best traders in the world treat events like budgets and elections as *opportunities*, not certainties. They size small. They hedge. They walk away with consistent profits over many events, not a home run in one.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Date events are tradable; open-ended uncertainty is not.** Only trade budgets, elections, and RBI policies where the date is fixed in advance.
- **The market reprices based on the gap between expectation and reality.** Study the consensus before the event. If everyone is bullish, the surprise will be bearish.
- **Use options for event trades, not futures.** Straddles capture volatility. Spreads reduce cost. Never carry naked futures across a major announcement.
- **Use FD interest to fund option bets.** If the trade fails, you lose only the interest, not your capital. If it wins, the returns can be 5x to 20x.
- **Exit by the close on event day.** The post-event gap is unpredictable. Take your profit and sleep well. There will always be another event next month.
