# Chapter 19: Trading Political Events – The Election Shock

## The Day the Screen Turned Red

On June 4, 2024, at 8:00 AM, I sat in my study with a cup of coffee that would go cold before I took a single sip. The Nifty futures were already down 2% in the pre-market session. By 10:00 AM, as the counting trends showed the BJP-led NDA falling short of expectations, the screen turned a shade of red I hadn't seen since March 2020.

By 11:30 AM, the Nifty had fallen 8.5% from the previous close. The index touched 21,281. People who had bought at the open were down 5% in two hours. People who had bought options the day before were staring at numbers that made no sense—unless they had bought puts.

Let me tell you what actually happened that day, and more importantly, what a prepared trader could have done about it.

---

## The Nature of Political Events

Political events are different from earnings or budget announcements. They have three characteristics that make them uniquely dangerous and potentially profitable:

**First**, they are binary in a way that corporate results are not. An earnings report can be 10% above or below estimates. An election result is either win or lose. The market reprices entire sectors—PSU banks, infrastructure, defense—in minutes based on who forms the government.

**Second**, the information cascade is brutal. In the 2024 election, the first trends at 8:30 AM showed the NDA leading in 290 seats. By 10:30 AM, that number had dropped to 240. By 12:00 PM, it was 230. Each downward revision triggered another wave of selling because institutional algorithms were programmed to reduce exposure to "continuity stocks" as the probability of a weak mandate increased.

**Third**, the recovery is rarely on the same day. Despite what hopeful headlines suggest, markets do not V-bottom from political shocks. The June 4, 2024 low was 21,281. The Nifty did hit 26,000 eventually—but only in late September, nearly four months later. Anyone who bought the dip at 22,000 thinking the recovery would be instant was sitting on losses for weeks.

## The Pre-Event Positioning Framework

You cannot trade political events without preparation. The day of the event is too late. Here is the framework I have used across multiple election cycles—2004, 2009, 2014, 2019, and 2024.

### Step 1: Assess the Base Case

Before any political event, you need to know what the market has already priced in. In June 2024, the consensus was clear: the BJP-led NDA would win 300+ seats. The Nifty was trading at 22,500, pricing in policy continuity. Any result below 272 seats (the majority mark) would be a shock.

How do you quantify this? Look at the options market. In the week before June 4, the Nifty 23,000 call option was trading at a premium that implied a 60% probability of the index crossing that level. The 21,500 put was trading at a premium that implied only a 20% probability of a fall below that level. The market was pricing in a smooth outcome.

This is your first warning sign. When the market is pricing in only one outcome, the risk of the alternative is actually higher than the option prices suggest—because option sellers are pricing based on recent history, not the full distribution of political outcomes.

### Step 2: Build the Hedge

Here is where I differ from most traders. I do not go into political events with directional bets. I go in with hedges.

**The Pre-Election Straddle**

On June 3, 2024, the Nifty was at 22,500. A trader could have bought:

- One lot of Nifty 22,500 call option (expiring June 6): ₹150 premium
- One lot of Nifty 22,500 put option (expiring June 6): ₹140 premium
- Total investment per lot: ₹290 × 50 = ₹14,500

The total premium paid is ₹14,500 per lot. This is the maximum loss. If the Nifty stays flat, you lose the entire premium. But if the Nifty moves 5% in either direction, the value explodes.

Let's work through the actual numbers from June 4:

At 11:30 AM, the Nifty was at 21,281—a fall of 5.4% from the strike price. The 22,500 put option, which cost ₹140 the day before, was now trading at approximately ₹1,200. That is a gain of ₹1,060 per share, or ₹53,000 per lot.

The call option, which cost ₹150, was now worth roughly ₹10—a loss of ₹140 per share.

Net P&L per lot: ₹53,000 (put profit) - ₹7,000 (call loss) = ₹46,000 profit on an investment of ₹14,500.

That is a 317% return in a single day.

But here's the important part: you do not need to be right about the direction. You just need to be right about the volatility. The straddle captures the move regardless of which way it goes.

### Step 3: The FD-Funded Option Trade

Not everyone wants to risk ₹14,500 on a binary event. There is a lower-risk way to participate.

Here is a strategy I have used for years. Instead of funding option trades from your capital, fund them from your fixed deposit interest.

Say you have ₹10 lakh in a fixed deposit earning 8% per year. That gives you ₹80,000 in annual interest. Use this interest—not the capital—to buy options for political events.

For the June 2024 election, you could have used ₹80,000 to buy:

- 5 lots of the Nifty 22,500 straddle: ₹14,500 × 5 = ₹72,500

If the event goes wrong, you lose only the ₹72,500—which is the interest you would have earned anyway. Your ₹10 lakh capital remains untouched. If the event creates volatility, as it did on June 4, your 5 lots would have returned ₹2,30,000—a 217% return on the interest you risked.

This is not a theoretical strategy. I have been doing this for every budget, election, and major monetary policy event since 2016. The FD interest acts as a natural buffer. You are trading with the bank's money, not your own.

## The Post-Event Analysis: What Actually Happened

That night, two events changed the trajectory. TDP's Chandrababu Naidu and JDU's Nitish Kumar pledged support to the NDA. The government would be formed. The uncertainty resolved.

But the recovery was not instant. The Nifty recovered from 21,285 to 22,500 over the next two weeks. It took four months to hit 26,000. In between, there were three corrections of 3-5% each as the market adjusted to the reality of a coalition government.

This is the part that most traders get wrong. They assume that once the binary event is resolved, the market goes back to where it was. It doesn't. Political events change the structure of the market. Sectors that benefited from strong majority expectations—PSU banks, infrastructure, defense—did not recover to their pre-election highs for months. Sectors that benefited from uncertainty—gold, FMCG, pharma—outperformed.

Here is a comparison of sectoral performance on June 4 vs. June 5:

| Sector | June 4 Change | June 5 Change | 1-Month Later |
|--------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| Nifty 50 | -5.9% | +3.2% | -2.1% |
| Nifty PSU Bank | -12.4% | +5.1% | -6.8% |
| Nifty Infra | -7.8% | +2.9% | -3.5% |
| Nifty FMCG | -2.1% | +1.5% | +4.2% |
| Nifty Pharma | -1.8% | +2.1% | +5.6% |

The pattern is clear. The sectors that had the biggest rally on strong majority expectations—PSU banks, infrastructure—saw the sharpest falls on election day. And even after a bounce on June 5, they continued to underperform for months. Meanwhile, defensive sectors that had lagged—FMCG, pharma—slowly regained ground.

This is the post-event play: do not try to catch falling knives in the "continuity" sectors. Instead, position for a rotation into defensive sectors that will benefit from policy uncertainty.

## The 2004 Lesson

For those who think 2024 was the only political shock, let me take you back to 2004.

In May 2004, the BJP-led NDA was expected to win re-election. The market had rallied 15% in the two months before the results. On May 13, counting day, the NDA lost. The Sensex fell 15% in a single day—the second-worst crash in Indian history at that point.

What happened next? The market did not recover for three years. The new UPA government, led by the Congress party, was seen as business-unfriendly. Foreign institutional investors pulled out ₹5,000 crore in the first week. The Sensex took from May 2004 to December 2007 to double from the crash low.

Compare that to 2024. The Nifty recovered its election loss in two weeks and went on to hit record highs. Why the difference? Because in 2004, the outcome was a genuine surprise that changed the policy direction of the country. In 2024, the outcome was a "surprise" only in the magnitude of the win—the government remained the same, just weakened.

This is the key distinction: a political shock that changes the party in power is more damaging than a shock that changes only the margin of victory. The former requires structural repositioning. The latter is just volatility.

## The Pre-Event Hedging Checklist

Based on my experience across five election cycles, here is the checklist I recommend:

**Seven Days Before the Event:**

- [ ] Identify the base case (what is the market pricing in?)
- [ ] Look at options implied volatility—if IV is below 20, the market is complacent
- [ ] Reduce directional exposure by 50%—sell stocks that would crash if the base case fails
- [ ] Calculate the FD interest available for option funding

**Three Days Before the Event:**

- [ ] Buy the at-the-money straddle with 3-5 days to expiry (captures event volatility)
- [ ] If the straddle is too expensive (IV above 30), buy an out-of-the-money put and call separately
- [ ] Keep cash reserve at 70% of portfolio—do not be fully invested

**Day of the Event:**

- [ ] Do not trade in the first 30 minutes—the opening is driven by news, not price discovery
- [ ] Watch for volume spikes: a 5x normal volume at a support or resistance level signals conviction
- [ ] If you must trade, use limit orders—stop-losses get hit in the chaos
- [ ] At 2:30 PM, decide: do you want overnight exposure? If no, square off all positions

**Post-Event (Next Day):**

- [ ] Analyze sectoral performance—which sectors recovered and which didn't?
- [ ] If the event resolved uncertainty, buy the sectors that were oversold on fear
- [ ] If the event created new uncertainty, add to defensive sectors (FMCG, pharma)
- [ ] Do not use leverage in the first week—volatility remains high

## The Worked Example: A Trader's P&L

Let me simulate two traders to show you the difference between preparation and hope.

**Trader A: The Directional Player**

Trader A believed the NDA would win easily. On June 3, he bought 5 lots of Nifty Futures at 22,500. Margin required: ₹4,50,000.

On June 4, as the Nifty fell, he held on, hoping for a bounce. At 11:30 AM, the Nifty hit 21,281. His position was down ₹6,09,500—more than his initial margin. He got a margin call and had to add ₹2,00,000 to maintain the position.

By close, the Nifty was at 21,285. His loss: ₹6,07,500. His total capital at risk: ₹6,50,000.

He held overnight. The next day, the Nifty opened at 22,200. He exited at 22,000. His profit on the bounce: ₹3,57,500. Net loss after two days: ₹2,50,000.

Total loss: ₹2,50,000 on a ₹6,50,000 capital base.

**Trader B: The Event Player**

Trader B bought 5 lots of the Nifty 22,500 straddle on June 3. Total premium: ₹72,500 (funded from FD interest).

On June 4, at 11:30 AM, the put options were worth ₹1,200 each. He closed both positions. Profit on puts: ₹5,30,000. Loss on calls: ₹35,000. Net profit: ₹4,95,000.

He did not trade the next day. He booked his profit and walked away.

Total profit: ₹4,95,000 on a ₹72,500 risk.

The difference is not luck. Trader B had a framework. Trader A had an opinion.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Political events require preparation, not prediction.** The pre-event straddle is the most reliable way to capture volatility without needing to guess direction. Fund it from FD interest to limit your capital risk.

2. **Volume is the only indicator that matters on event day.** A 5x volume spike at a key level signals that large money is moving. Do not fade these moves unless you have a strong reason.

3. **The recovery from political shocks is rarely instant.** The 2024 election saw the Nifty take four months to hit new highs. The 2004 election took three years. Do not buy the dip on day one and expect a V-shaped recovery.

4. **Sector rotation is the post-event play.** Sectors that rallied on strong majority expectations will underperform after a weak mandate. Defensive sectors (FMCG, pharma) will outperform. Position accordingly.

5. **The most dangerous position to hold during a political event is a large directional bet.** The margin calls will force you to exit at the worst possible time. If you must hold, size your position so that a 10% move against you does not trigger a margin call.
