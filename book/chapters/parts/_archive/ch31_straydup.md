# Chapter 31: Crypto as a Satellite — Not a Religion

If any single asset class divided market participants across the years 2018–2026, it was cryptocurrency. Bitcoin went from under $4,000 in 2018 to a peak near $69,000 in November 2021, collapsed to below $18,000 in the 2022 crypto winter, and recovered gradually into the $80,000–100,000 range by 2025–2026. Ethereum followed a similar arc, from under $100 to nearly $5,000 and back. Those swings produced life-changing gains for some and devastating losses for others.

The position taken throughout this book is simple: **crypto belongs in a portfolio, but as a satellite allocation — never the core.** The numbers, strategies, and India-specific tax facts that follow explain how a trader can participate without gambling the farm.

## Why 5–10% of Capital Is Enough

The case for any crypto allocation rests on one argument: it is an uncorrelated asset with high volatility, and a small position can meaningfully boost a portfolio's total return without putting the rest at risk. The case against a larger allocation rests on at least five arguments, all valid:

1. **India taxes crypto punitively.** Gains on crypto assets are taxed at a flat 30% with no deduction for expenses and no basic exemption. Worse, a 1% Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) applies to every transaction — so a daily trader who turns over ₹1 crore in a year has ₹10 lakh tied up as TDS credits until the tax filing refund, even if their net profit is zero.
2. **The regulatory status remains uncertain.** India's Cryptocurrency and Regulation of Official Digital Currency Bill, introduced in 2021, created a regulatory shadow that has never fully lifted. Exchanges operate in a grey area; banking access has been intermittently blocked; and the tax regime explicitly excludes crypto from the business-income treatment that F&O traders enjoy on indices.
3. **Exchange risk is real.** The collapse of FTX in November 2022, which wiped out billions of dollars in customer funds, proved that even the largest platforms are not too big to fail. Counterparty risk in crypto is an order of magnitude higher than in regulated exchanges.
4. **Volatility destroys undercapitalised positions.** A 15-minute candle that moves Bitcoin by $2,000 would be an extraordinary event in any equity market. In crypto it happens regularly. Without position sizing, one such candle liquidates a poorly margined account.
5. **The mental bandwidth cost is high.** Monitoring crypto positions around the clock — especially when the 1% TDS rule penalises frequent trading — burns attention that a trader could deploy more productively in other markets.

For these reasons, experienced crypto traders consistently size their positions at 5–10% of total capital, treating the allocation as pocket-money risk that cannot sink the main portfolio even if it goes to zero.

## What India's Tax Regime Means in Practice

To understand how India's crypto tax works, consider a concrete example. A trader executes ten round-trip trades on Bitcoin during a financial year, each of ₹1,00,000 notional value. Their total turnover is ₹10,00,000. At a 1% TDS rate, ₹10,000 is deducted and remitted to the tax department.

If the trader's net profit after all trades is ₹50,000, the tax due is 30% × ₹50,000 = ₹15,000 — but the trader has already paid ₹10,000 in TDS, so the net outlay at filing time is only ₹5,000. However, if the trades were frequent enough that total turnover was ₹1 crore, the TDS tied up is ₹1 lakh — a cash-flow drag that can meaningfully constrain a small account.

The 30%/1% structure effectively makes **intraday trading in crypto a loss-making proposition from a cash-flow standpoint** for all but the largest accounts. For most participants, the rational approach is to hold long-term (more than three years, potentially benefitting from LTCG treatment where the levy drops to 10% if indexation is favourable — though the law on this has been contested) or to use a registered crypto F&O exchange where the income is treated as business income with full expense offset and loss set-off across instruments.

## Crypto Options: Opportunity in High IV

The one area where crypto genuinely rewards disciplined participants is **option selling**. Cryptocurrency derivatives trade with implied volatility that is 2–10× higher than equivalent equity index options. A far out-of-the-money put on the Nifty that pays ₹10 in premium might pay ₹200 on Bitcoin at the same moneyness. This is not free money — crypto can and does move to the strikes that the premium priced in — but it means that the mechanics of range-bound premium selling work as well in crypto as they do in indices, with one critical adjustment: **breakeven bands must be wider.**

A typical Bitcoin option structure during a consolidation period might work as follows. Between mid-2025 and early 2026, Bitcoin traded in a broad $80,000–$100,000 range. A short straddle at a $90,000 strike, combining a sold call and a sold put, would have collected a premium in the range of several thousand dollars per contract on the global exchanges, while the highest-reach exchange offering crypto F&O to Indian residents would have offered comparable margins. The breakeven for the structure — the distance the price could move in either direction before the position lost money — would have been wide enough to span the entire historical range. The trade would profit as long as Bitcoin did not break decisively outside its established consolidation band.

Ratio spreads and butterflies also work in crypto. A call butterfly during the same period — buying a call at the lower end of the range, selling two at the midpoint, and buying one at the upper end — would have offered defined risk and a wide profit zone, with the final leg capped by the fly's construction. The key rule: in crypto's high-IV environment, the structure must be built with such wide strikes that the underlying can swing violently without reaching the breakeven.

## Sizing and Execution

The exchanges available to Indian participants evolved significantly between 2022 and 2026. The FTX collapse of November 2022 triggered a flight to regulated platforms. By 2024–2026, at least one platform had obtained FIU (Financial Intelligence Unit) registration in India, allowing residents to trade crypto options in rupees without foreign-exchange exposure. On such platforms, margins are set in INR, profits are taxed as business income (netting across coins, offset against expenses), and the need to handle TCS (Tax Collected at Source) on crypto funding is eliminated.

The structures taught in Part IV of this book — short straddles and strangles for range-bound markets, ratio spreads for mildly directional views, butterflies for defined-risk event trades — all translate directly to crypto options. The only changes are:
- **Size smaller:** 5–10% of capital, not more.
- **Breakevens wider:** at least 2× the recent daily range.
- **Exchange risk:** prioritise FIU-registered platforms over offshore ones.
- **Tax consequence:** treat crypto F&O as business income; maintain separate books.

At the margins it covers, crypto option selling has produced consistent monthly returns — not because the market is easy, but because the IV is high enough to compensate sellers for the genuine risk of wild swings. That is the entire thesis: a disciplined premium seller, in any asset class, makes money by charging insurance premiums that reflect the market's fear. In crypto, the fear is real, and the premiums are proportionally large. The job of the trader is to keep the allocation small enough that even a total loss does not change their lifestyle.

> **Key Takeaways**
> * Crypto belongs as a 5–10% satellite allocation — never the core — given its tax, regulatory, and exchange risks.
> * India's 30% flat tax + 1% TDS makes intraday crypto trading structurally penalised; holding or using a registered F&O platform is preferable.
> * Crypto's high implied volatility (2–10× equity indices) rewards range-bound premium selling — but breakevens must be 2× wider than usual.
> * Short straddles, ratio spreads and butterflies all work in crypto; the constructs from Part IV translate directly.
> * Exchange risk is the single greatest danger in crypto. Use FIU-registered platforms; size so that a full platform freeze does not damage your main portfolio.
