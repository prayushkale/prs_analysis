## Key lessons
- [2788] The HNI entry method: park the purchase money in debt funds, sell a put at your target (3,000PE at 27 rupees); if assigned, you buy at your price having earned interest plus premium; if not, the premium is income.
- [2780] Because the protected position cannot lose, you can ladder new collars every further 5% of market fall using debt-fund tranches (20% at -10%, then each -5%) - courage without conviction-destroying drawdowns.
- [2788] TCS: 75% promoter-held cash cow funding the group via dividends; PE compressed from ~40 to ~21-22 as the stock fell ~30-35% from 4,500 toward 3,000 - below 3,000 it becomes a buy-the-dips value candidate.
- [2780] A collar funded by equal premiums (buy 25,000PE, sell 28,000CE on Dec-2026) gives zero downside and ~3,400 points of capped upside (~13-14% over ~17 months) - slightly better than FD with full protection.
- [2788] When 92% of FIIs are net short index futures and sentiment is universally bearish, good news becomes rocket fuel - the May 2025 ceasefire produced a ~4% single-day rally from maximum pessimism.
- [2780] India charges span + exposure + extra >9-month margin even on hedged ETF positions, where the US charges nothing for covered calls - friction that keeps zero-risk capital out of the market.
- [0210] A durable, dominant media franchise can trade at a fraction of peer multiples when saddled with a legal/political overhang - the discount itself is the opportunity if the overhang clears.
- [1131] A warrant is functionally a long-dated call: pay premium now, exercise at a fixed price until expiry, maximum loss capped at premium - but with issuer/liquidity risk and thin markets.
- [0135] Vaccine/normalization news deflates the fear premium: metals crashed the day after Russia's August 2020 vaccine announcement while old-economy stocks with 'bad results' rallied.
- [1118] Every centralized crypto exchange carries custody/counterparty risk; there is no independent depository to verify holdings, so registration jurisdiction and regulation matter.
