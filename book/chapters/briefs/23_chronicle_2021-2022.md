## Key lessons
- [1098] Cash-market holdings and F&O margins are not linked in India: a fully-hedged covered call still pays full span margin, unlike the US - a structural cost of selling covered calls in India.
- [0541] Markets rarely repeat the same fall for the same reason: second COVID wave took 1,200 points where the first took 40% - so 2013's 20% taper crash should not be the default expectation.
- [1105] An aggressively skewed put ladder (long far put financed by selling nearer puts) expresses a mildly bullish view with a known floor - define the breakeven before the event, not after.
- [0927] A 70-point futures premium with a week left in the series is a covering signature: such distortions mean the move is mechanical, not convictional - expect mean reversion of the basis.
- [0347] Good news is good news; very good news is bad news; bad news is bad news; very bad news is good news — position by what the news does to policy expectations, not to the economy.
- [1413] Rule for the year-ahead view: below a broken 18,000, buy quality or simply Nifty BeES - but always with a hedge (his example: long futures + far put financed by a short call)
- [1089] When a market rallies 700 points and futures shorts barely cover, the crowd conviction is bearish - the fuel for any squeeze is measured by what remains, not what covered.
- [1160] Max-pain positioning is a contrarian map: when everyone sells 17.5-17.7 calls, either it is a wall of resistance or the writers are wrong - historically writers are wrong
- [0362] When a majority (60%+) is short, falls are cushioned by profit-taking and rallies are amplified by stop-loss buying - the asymmetry behind the FII-positioning indicator.
- [0416] If a move looks too good to be true (1,000% in a year), stay away regardless of momentum. Index damage from a scandal is usually contained, but collateral dips happen.
