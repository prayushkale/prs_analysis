## Core lessons
1. In violent moves, premiums behave abnormally because losing-side sellers are forced to square up and roll - not because the market 'should' behave that way.
2. Never deploy all margin in a strangle; spare cash lets you sell closer strikes without buying back the far leg at the worst price.
3. Short strangle is called the most dangerous strategy - unlimited one-sided loss with both legs naked.
4. Margin-release mechanics (square far leg, sell nearer leg) explain bid-ask distortions during panics.
5. Prices move on results versus expectations, not on absolute results - better-than-feared can rally
6. On expiry day, stocks can move opposite to results as shorts cover
7. Never copy someone's strategy blindly unless you have their capital and skills (bus-pothole analogy)
8. Without extra capital and adjustment skills, your only tool is a stop-loss in your mind

## Strategies
- **Strangle roll-under-stress mechanics**: Short 9,000CE + 7,000PE written Friday around spot 8,000; on a violent rally, buy back 7,000PE (releasing margin) and se
- **IndusInd earnings-day iron-fly style combo**: Sell 400CE + sell 400PE, buy 500CE + buy 300PE (current month)
