# Compiler Brief: "Wealth Beyond the Screen" (Chapters 29–31)

**Part Overview:** This part moves from screen-driven trading to enduring wealth creation. It covers macro events, fundamental company analysis, and the psychology required to stay the course. Verified market shocks (election crash, HDFC Bank meltdown, GST overhaul) serve as real-world anchors, while transcript IDs provide expert commentary and case studies.

## Chapter 29: The Big Picture – Navigating Macro Shocks

### Purpose
Teach how political, regulatory, and economic events move markets, and how to assess their long-term impact rather than react short-term.

### Key Sources (transcript IDs)
- **1062** – Detailed interview on the June 4, 2024 election crash and recovery timeline.
- **2442** – Macroeconomic analysis linking GST slab changes to sector rotation.
- **0230** – Expert breakdown of how coalition governments affect market sentiment.
- **0193** – Case study of 2024 rally vs. previous election years (tables of returns).
- **1820** – Discussion on using macro indicators (e.g., GDP, fiscal deficit) to anticipate policy shifts.

### Verified Facts (anchor events)
- **Event 1 (corrected):** On June 4, 2024, Nifty fell ~8.5% intraday (low 21,281), closed ~-6% as BJP-led NDA looked short of majority. TDP/JDU support formed government; Nifty then recovered and hit record highs 32 times in 2024, reaching ~26,000 in late September.
- **Event 4 (corrected, partial):** India overhauled GST slabs effective September 22, 2025 – use this to discuss regulatory risk and sector winners/losers.

### Enhancement Tasks
- **Worked example:** Portfolio impact simulation – investor who sold on June 4 vs. one who held through recovery (calculate gains from low to high).
- **Table:** Comparison of major election-day crashes (2004, 2009, 2014, 2024) – depth, recovery time, subsequent returns.

Medium

## Chapter 30: The Company Behind the Ticker – Fundamental Pitfalls

### Purpose
Show how company-specific events (earnings misses, sector headwinds) cause sharp losses, and how to use fundamental analysis to avoid such traps.

### Key Sources (transcript IDs)
- **0909** – Deep dive into HDFC Bank’s Q3 FY24 results and the subsequent 8.5% crash.
- **0116** – Banking sector risk factors: NIM compression, deposit competition, regulatory changes.
- **0598** – Interview with a value investor on identifying overvalued stocks using P/E and P/B.
- **0354** – Case study of a small-cap stock that crashed after promoter pledge revelation.
- **2043** – Step-by-step guide to reading quarterly earnings reports and spotting red flags.

### Verified Facts (anchor events)
- **Event 2 (verified):** On January 17, 2024, HDFC Bank shares plunged 8.5% (worst since COVID crash) after disappointing Q3 results and US Fed signals. Investors lost ₹1 lakh crore in one day. Nifty/Sensex had worst day since June 2022.

### Enhancement Tasks
- **Worked example:** Pre- vs. post-crash fundamental metrics for HDFC Bank (ROE, NIM, net profit growth, asset quality ratios) – show how the stock was not cheap before the fall.
- **Table:** Checklist of five warning signs in quarterly results (revenue vs. estimates, provisions, management guidance etc.).

### Complexity Estimate
Medium–Hard

### Length Target
3,000 words

## Chapter 31: The Mind Game – Mastering Psychology for Wealth

### Purpose
Address behavioral biases (loss aversion, herding, recency) that destroy long-term returns, and provide discipline frameworks to hold through volatility.

### Key Sources (transcript IDs)
- **1380** – Behavioral finance expert on why investors panic-sold on June 4, 2024.
- **0844** – Interview with a fund manager on maintaining conviction during sector crashes (e.g., HDFC Bank).
- **1589** – Trader who turned ₹5 lakh into ₹1 crore over 10 years – psychology of patience.
- **0094** – Discussion on confirmation bias during GST overhaul announcements.
- **0908** – Real amortization table showing cost of mistimed exits (using election day data).

### Verified Facts (anchor events)
- **Event 1 (election crash & recovery)** – Used to illustrate the cost of panic selling vs. holding.
- **Event 2 (HDFC Bank crash)** – Example of a company-specific shock where overreaction later gave buying opportunities.
- **Event 4 (GST overhaul)** – How investors overreacted to policy news, ignoring long-term structural benefits.

### Enhancement Tasks
- **Worked example:** Calculate total wealth difference for an investor who sold HDFC Bank at the bottom vs. one who held and averaged down (use actual price recovery: HDFC Bank recovered ~12% from low in 3 months).
- **Table:** Common biases with real Indian market examples (e.g., recency – assuming June 4 crash meant bear market).

### Complexity Estimate
Medium

### Length Target
2,500 words
