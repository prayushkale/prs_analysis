## Key lessons
- [1086] India's 60-80% valuation premium to emerging peers makes it first in line for selling in a global crisis; growth-stage analogy: a child grown from 1ft to 6ft won't reach 36ft.
- [0112] The right time to invest is when you have surplus cash; the right time to exit is when you need the money - waiting for the 'right opportunity' is itself a losing strategy.
- [0119] Euphoria screen: if no Nifty-50 stock yields more than bank FDs, the market is euphoric; in 2020 ITC/ONGC/IOC/GAIL/Vedanta yielded above FDs, so the stage was skepticism.
- [0112] Post-crash lows of true panic candles (2008, 2020) are historically not retested once the recovery candle's high is crossed - drawdown bottoming behaves asymmetrically.
- [0072] Resting limit orders far from spot can get filled by flash spikes; square off immediately - such dislocations vanish in seconds and cannot be systematically captured.
- [1086] If FIIs sold a third of their ~$290 billion India exposure, the price and rupee damage would be systemic; equity returns must beat FD returns to justify the risk.
- [0250] A GameStop squeeze is near-impossible in India: borrowing is illiquid, circuits halt cascades, SEBI raises margins dynamically, and position limits force bans.
- [1086] Black swans are rare, explainable only in hindsight, and severe - and they can be positive (2019's surprise corporate-tax cut: 30% to 22% in one day).
- [0112] The depth of the crash sets the pace of recovery: 2008's >50% fall doubled in a year; 2020's shallower fall implies a slower but persistent recovery.
- [0208] Divided US government stalls fiscal stimulus and keeps global markets uncertain; unified government (with VP tie-break) can unlock a fresh rally leg.
