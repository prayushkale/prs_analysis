## Key Lessons
- Markets can digest good news and bad news - what they cannot digest is uncertainty.
- Results move stocks by expectation gap, not absolute quality - the motorbike-versus-apple analogy.
- Use overnight ADR moves of listed peers to estimate next-day reactions to results.
- Markets reward the removal of uncertainty more than the news itself.
- VIX measures fear, not direction - it can spike in rallies when option sellers sweat.
- Anchor put-selling to volume-profile high-volume nodes, not round numbers.
- Max pain marks where option buyers hurt most - heavy OI plus max pain pins the expiry range.
- Everything known is already priced in; judge PSU banks by results trend, not headlines.
- Consolidation with volatility is healthy - it defuses future corrections.
- FIIs buy in bursts after pausing to bait short sellers; watch F&O positioning, not just cash.
- Very-short-term sentiment is set by FII behavior in F&O, not by cash-market flows.
- A strong pre-market plus against weak global cues predicts a big minus - and vice versa.
- Short covering is mechanical: when news flips, the weak side runs and the crowd joins the beating.
- Sell calls into falls and puts into rallies symmetrically - VIX declines cushion reversals.
- In the very short term, results move prices via positioning (short covering vs long unwinding) - fundamentals work only
