## Part 6: A Market Chronicle 2018-2026

### Chapter 1: 2018–2020 – The Crack and the Crash
**Purpose:** To document the stress cycle that broke the NPA cleanup myth, and the pandemic collapse that forced a monetary revolution.

**Key Verified Facts:**  
- **IL&FS default (Sept 2018):** Triggered a liquidity freeze in the NBFC and HFC sector.  
- **Yes Bank rescue (March 2020):** RBI imposed a moratorium; SBI led a reconstruction scheme.  
- **COVID-19 crash (March 2020):** Nifty fell ~38% in a month; Sensex hit 25,638.  
- **Emergency fiscal-measures (March–May 2020):** Free LPG, PMGKY, and the first tranche of the Atma Nirbhar Bharat package.

**Narrative Arc:** The chapter opens with the IL&FS failure—a "Lehman moment" that exposed fragility in shadow banking. Liquidity contagion spreads to Yes Bank, forcing central intervention. Then COVID-19 arrives: a sudden, global stop. The arc turns from fear to survival as the government and RBI deploy unprecedented liquidity, lay the groundwork for a V-shaped recovery, and close the chapter with the first signs of retail investor entry.

### Chapter 2: 2021–2022 – The Euphoria and the Whipsaw
**Purpose:** To capture the unprecedented retail boom, the IPO craze, and the shock of a hot war that ended the pandemic-era rally.

**Key Verified Facts:**  
- **Retail Demat accounts cross 70 million (2021):** Zerodha, Groww, and Angel One lead a surge in first-time investors.  
- **Zomato, Paytm, Nykaa IPOs (2021):** Zomato listed at 53% premium; Paytm crashed 27% on debut.  
- **Russia-Ukraine war (Feb 2022):** Nifty fell 10% in a week; crude hit $130; FIIs pulled ₹2.1 lakh crore.  
- **Adani rally peaks (Sept 2022):** Group stocks gained 4x–8x; market cap crossed ₹19 lakh crore.

**Narrative Arc:** The chapter begins with the "new India" euphoria—retail investors buying every dip, IPOs oversubscribed, and a belief that markets only go up. The scorpion sting arrives with Russia-Ukraine, commodity shock, and brutal FII selling. The arc bends again as Adani stocks defy gravity, setting the stage for the next chapter’s reckoning.

### Chapter 3: 2023–2024 – The Reckoning and the Reset
**Purpose:** To examine the destruction of the Adani narrative, the return of FIIs, and the structural shift toward high-quality, regulated growth.

**Key Verified Facts:**  
- **Hindenburg report (Jan 2023):** Adani Group lost ₹12 lakh crore in market cap in days; Supreme Court panel appointed.  
- **RBI’s credit tightening (2023):** Risk weights on consumer credit and NBFCs raised to cool unsecured lending.  
- **Record FII net buying of ₹1.87 lakh crore (2023–24):** Driven by strong GDP growth (8.2% Q3 FY24) and stable earnings.  
- **Election stability (June 2024):** BJP-led NDA returns to power, markets rally 3.5% on result day despite exit poll misses.

**Narrative Arc:** The arc opens with the Hindenburg bombshell—a short-seller attack that vaporised a star promoter’s wealth and tested Indian regulatory credibility. From that rubble, the market pivots: FIIs return, attracted by disciplined macro numbers and stable policy after the election. The chapter ends with a "flight to quality"—investors favour largecaps and regulated banks over promoter-driven stories.

### Chapter 4: 2025–2026 – The Maturity Test
**Purpose:** To project how structural reforms, global fragmentation, and a maturing retail base will shape Indian equities and drive valuation reassessment.

**Key Verified Facts:**  
- **AI & manufacturing capex cycle deepens:** PLI schemes for semiconductors, electronics, and defence attract foreign committed investment of over $20 billion.  
- **Demat accounts cross 150 million (mid-2025):** Monthly SIP flows reach ₹22,000–25,000 crore.  
- **Global tariff wars & fractured supply chains:** Indian port and export data show high volatility; China+1 strategy accelerates but faces new scrutiny.  
- **Nifty valuation compression (2026):** Price-to-earnings re-rates from ~22x to ~17x as liquidity normalises and global risk-free rates stabilise at ~4.5%.

**Narrative Arc:** The chapter begins with hope—a new, domestically driven capex cycle backed by policy certainty and tech adoption. The middle falters as global headwinds (tariffs, delayed rate cuts) compress multiples. The arc resolves with a quiet maturing: SIPs become a structural anchor, retail losses teach discipline, and the market sheds its "emerging" volatility for a steadier, compounding rhythm. The chronicle closes on a note of cautious, long-term conviction.
