## Key Lessons (by frequency)
- [1×] Before a binary event, map both outcomes: if the expected outcome is already priced, upside is capped while the surprise is uncapped.
- [1×] Traders buy strength and sell weakness; investors must take the opposite side.
- [1×] Dividend yield above FD rate lets you wait years for a re-rating ('every dog has its day').
- [1×] When even fund managers refuse new money in a hot segment, froth is real.
- [1×] Post-shock outcomes can invert: a crash that brings the 'bad' outcome often starts the next bull run (2016 US parallel).
- [1×] Never trust exit poll or opinion poll results - always expect the unexpected
- [1×] When everybody expects a win, the win is priced in. Buy puts before the event because the downside is bigger than the upside
- [1×] When IV is very high before a binary event, a reverse calendar (sell near, buy far) profits from the post-event IV crush

## Strategy Setups
- **Election result hedge**: Buy put option before election results; sell a lower strike put to create spread
  - Risk: Premium decay if no volatility
  - Best regime: Before binary events where consensus is bullish
- **Reverse Call Calendar**: Sell near-month call option, buy far-month call option at same strike
  - Risk: If market makes a big sustained move, the far-month long call protects
  - Best regime: Before binary events when IV is elevated
- **Long-dated Nifty short strangle (annual range)**: Sold December-2022 15,000 put and 21,000 call
  - Risk: Both sides breached in an extreme year; margin lock-up for 12 months
  - Best regime: Wide VIX-implied annual range with no intent to predict direction
- **Long-dated short straddle + wings (position disclosed)**: Sold December-expiry 19,000 and 18,000 short straddles; sold 16,000 put and 20,000 call as wings
  - Risk: big directional move breaks the straddle; wings cap tail loss
  - Best regime: declining VIX, sideways-to-down market
- **Interest-only equity exposure (HNI framework)**: Keep 1 crore in FD/debt (~7 lakh/yr interest); invest only the interest - e.g., buy ATM calls after 10%+ market corrections each y
  - Risk: sideways markets burn the premium yearly (China example); psychologically hard
  - Best regime: capital-preservation-first investors in uncertain markets
