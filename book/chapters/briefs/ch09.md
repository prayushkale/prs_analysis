## Key Lessons
- For every option bought one is sold - if buying were strictly better, no seller would exist.
- Seller pays margin as collateral (FDs/shares), so cash outlay can be near zero; buyer pays premium in cash.
- Probability decides long-run winners: buyer wins in only one of five market scenarios, seller in most.
- Sellers can adjust/hedge a losing position (short futures, sell calls); a buyer has no equivalent fix.
- Little money and losable: buy. Large capital and steady profit: sell.
- An option is asymmetric betting: buyer risks only the premium; seller pays 1:1 per point beyond the strike.

## Strategies
- Collateral-funded option selling (framework): Sell options against collateral: bank FD, shares, mutual funds or bank guarantee pledged to the brok  (Buyer pays 2,000-3,000 premium cash vs seller margin ~80,000-90,000 (as stated) )
- Cash Secured Put: Sell a put at the strike where you want to own the stock; keep the full cash ready for delivery  (Bajaj Finance 1800PE sold at Rs.100 (2 lots, Rs.50,000 collected); Nifty Apr 750)
- OTM credit-sale adjustment on a losing ratio: Existing: long 350CE, short 2x 380CE (max loss ~0.50/share ~ Rs 800/lot). Adjustment: sell 1x 400CE   (Pre-adjustment loss ~Rs 3,000; post-adjustment profit ~Rs 2,000 per lot)
