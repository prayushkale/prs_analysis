## Key Lessons
- Delta equivalence is not payoff equivalence: two ATM calls match a future's delta but accumulate time decay; the synthet
- On any single day the two calls may lose less than the future - the difference appears over weeks of churn, when decay c
- Greeks act together; judging a position by one Greek (delta) is the most common retail options error.
- Selling a put profits in 4 of 5 scenarios (~80%); buying a call profits in 1 of 5 (~20%) - the structural seller's edge.
- Seller's cushion = premium collected: loss begins only beyond strike minus premium.
- Option buying can be 'operation successful, patient dead' - the view lands and the position still loses.
- Selling a call is likewise not buying a put; the asymmetry is identical on the bearish side.
- Delta = change in option price per 1-point move in the underlying: ATM ~0.5, deep ITM ~1, far OTM near 0.
- Call deltas run 0 to +1; put deltas 0 to -1.
- Cheap far-OTM options carry tiny deltas - their absolute upside even in a big rally is small; low price is not low risk 
- Deep-ITM options (delta ~1) behave like a synthetic future.
- Option prices never move by delta alone - gamma, theta, vega and rho bend real-world moves (ATM call gained 45 on a 114-
- Delta is the speed, gamma the acceleration — violent news makes ATM options outrun 0.5 delta.
- Gamma damage compounds near expiry: a 50-rupee ATM premium can double on a 100-point move.
- Option sellers avoid holding naked positions into expiry precisely because of gamma.
