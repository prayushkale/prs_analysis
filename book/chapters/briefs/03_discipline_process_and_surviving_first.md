## Key lessons
- [2802] Ask three questions before paying: total charges, recurring charges (the 72,000/year software trap that ate half a 2-lakh account), and the trainer's own audited trading record - MCA filings cost 100 rupees.
- [2802] Scams evolve (digital arrest, romance-crypto pig butchering via matrimonial sites); the defense is always the same: verify identity live, verify custody of money, never transfer to third-party accounts.
- [2714] Having nothing to lose is a strategic advantage: no commitments means freedom to venture into unfamiliar markets, industries and countries (genericizable to career and trading risk-taking).
- [2737] Call ratio fits a mild bullish view with a defined resistance: long one ATM call, short two far-OTM calls - profit concentrated below resistance, loss capped above it by design asymmetry.
- [0585] Stop-loss-market orders are structurally dangerous near crowded strikes: when everyone's trigger fires at once, your fill lands wherever liquidity is - within the day's legal price band.
- [0216] F&O profits above ~5 crore in a personal account can fall into the ~42% effective slab while a corporate structure is taxed at ~25% - entity choice materially changes post-tax returns.
- [0304] A stock makes 90% of its move in 10% of the time - boredom is the price of holding (Reliance flat 2008-2017, then +400% in 3 years; a power stock flat a decade, then 3x in 3 months).
- [0304] Trainers monetize the desperate: overpriced software (Rs60,000/yr, Rs30,000 commission) and fictitious model portfolios (Rs3L to Rs40L, '2000%') target people who just lost 90%.
- [2802] Returns are proportional to risk; the extreme example (buy a revolver for 1 lakh, extort 100 crore) shows the only way to promised returns - the missing variable is jail time.
- [2802] The math test: Indian businesses rarely clear 25-30% p.a. - anyone promising 100-200% from a weekend course is selling fantasy; even the trainer's own P&L (if filed) shows it.
