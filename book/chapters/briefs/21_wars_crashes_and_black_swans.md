## Key lessons
- [0135] Vaccine/normalization news deflates the fear premium: metals crashed the day after Russia's August 2020 vaccine announcement while old-economy stocks with 'bad results' rallied.
- [1086] India's 60-80% valuation premium to emerging peers makes it first in line for selling in a global crisis; growth-stage analogy: a child grown from 1ft to 6ft won't reach 36ft.
- [0845] Every ~2 years a crisis hits markets (Lehman 2008, Greece 2011, taper 2013, China 2015, demonetization 2016, Brexit 2017, COVID 2020) yet long-term India story stayed intact.
- [2660] In crisis weeks the goal is capital preservation, not profit: sell far out-of-the-money options where premium compensates (e.g., ~6% OTM puts paying ~5-6% for 3 days).
- [2652] A trader's book can note that the speaker treated the attack as a national-security event, not yet a market event; the market-only treatment follows in later videos.
- [2686] Economic interdependence is deterrence: hold your rival's bonds, employ its people, and war becomes economically irrational (Singapore model, Germany unification).
- [1086] If FIIs sold a third of their ~$290 billion India exposure, the price and rupee damage would be systemic; equity returns must beat FD returns to justify the risk.
- [0685] FII flows track election odds: 2018-19 they sold on hung-parliament fear until a geopolitical event flipped sentiment — watch state-election season the same way.
- [2660] Across 25 years of Indo-Pak crises Nifty fell roughly 4-6% each time; use the base rate, but remember coincident crises (dotcom, Lehman) distort attribution.
- [0239] Bad news can be pre-priced: check sector behavior that contradicts the obvious reaction (IT fell despite a weaker-rupee benefit) to see if damage is done.
