## Key Lessons
- Markets bottom after big panic selling (capitulation); overreaction to one name often marks a multi-week low.
- Panic in one bond/stock drags the whole sector down with it - contagion is mechanical, not fundamental.
- Traders who understand irrationality can profit during it; accumulation often starts at maximum pessimism.
- For every option bought one is sold - if buying were strictly better, no seller would exist.
- Seller pays margin as collateral (FDs/shares), so cash outlay can be near zero; buyer pays premium in cash.
- Probability decides long-run winners: buyer wins in only one of five market scenarios, seller in most.

## Strategies
- Collateral-funded option selling (framework): Sell options against collateral: bank FD, shares, mutual funds or bank guarantee pledged to the brok  (Buyer pays 2,000-3,000 premium cash vs seller margin ~80,000-90,000 (as stated) )
- Strike-and-expiry put ladder (illustrative, high risk): Sold puts at 6,500 / 7,000 / 7,300 / 7,400 / 7,500 / 7,800 / 8,500 across weekly, May and June expir  (Capital ~95 lakh -> ~45 lakh profit in 6 sessions (~50%); sold Jun 7,000PE @475,)
- Multi-strike, multi-expiry put selling (April 2020): Sold put options spread across various strike prices and expiry periods rather than one strike  (7500PE sold around Rs.75 during a morning dip; SGX Nifty ~9200 vs ~9000 close)
