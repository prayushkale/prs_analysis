## Core lessons
1. Markets bottom after big panic selling (capitulation); overreaction to one name often marks a multi-week low.
2. Panic in one bond/stock drags the whole sector down with it - contagion is mechanical, not fundamental.
3. Traders who understand irrationality can profit during it; accumulation often starts at maximum pessimism.
4. For every option bought one is sold - if buying were strictly better, no seller would exist.
5. Seller pays margin as collateral (FDs/shares), so cash outlay can be near zero; buyer pays premium in cash.
6. Probability decides long-run winners: buyer wins in only one of five market scenarios, seller in most.
7. Sellers can adjust/hedge a losing position (short futures, sell calls); a buyer has no equivalent fix.
8. Little money and losable: buy. Large capital and steady profit: sell.

## Strategies
- **Collateral-funded option selling (framework)**: Sell options against collateral: bank FD, shares, mutual funds or bank guarantee pledged to the broker
- **Strike-and-expiry put ladder (illustrative, high risk)**: Sold puts at 6,500 / 7,000 / 7,300 / 7,400 / 7,500 / 7,800 / 8,500 across weekly, May and June expiries; small stock-opt
- **Multi-strike, multi-expiry put selling (April 2020)**: Sold put options spread across various strike prices and expiry periods rather than one strike
- **Monthly short option + weekly protective hedge**: Sell far-month option; buy near-week option slightly OTM below breakeven; re-buy weekly
- **Collateral-funded option selling (HNI pattern)**: Pledge debt mutual funds/bank FDs as collateral; sell options against the margin; keep interest as primary return
