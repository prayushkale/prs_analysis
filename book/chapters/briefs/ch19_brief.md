## Key Lessons
- Markets often bottom after a day of extreme volatility combined with a double bottom.
- Markets recover when the last bull turns bear - universal bearishness marks short-term bottoms.
- High VIX with flat close-to-close moves means violent intraday swings inside a range - the option seller's regime map.
- Max-OI put strikes show option sellers' conviction levels about support.
- Falling VIX on a flat/down day explains why option premiums decay on both sides - a seller's tailwind.
- Crude falls lift autos, OMCs and banks together - a rare broad-based Indian rally trigger.
- Pre-expiry, max open-interest strikes define a tradeable floor and ceiling for the day.
- Retail SIP flows keep absorbing FII selling - flows matter more than headlines.
- Good domestic data cannot override a fresh global supply shock - check what drove the last hour.
- A V-shaped recovery day after a capitulation often marks the end of the down-leg.
- Huge two-way volatility between two levels usually resolves in a violent breakout.
- Previous day's average traded price (VWAP) acts as intraday support/resistance.
- Option sellers can pre-commit to an expiry range from max-OI strikes and hedge the tails.
- Ahead of long weekends traders refuse to carry positions - square-off flows amplify moves.
- Trade-war headlines move index futures within minutes - fade confirmation, not the first headline.
