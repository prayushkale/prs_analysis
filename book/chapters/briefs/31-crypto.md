## Core lessons
1. Frequency decides classification: a few crypto trades a year is investing (capital gains); hundreds is trading (business income).
2. Crypto trading is not speculative income — you receive actual tokens, unlike F&O — so business-expense deductions apply.
3. Mining is a business: electricity and incidentals are deductible, computers depreciated as capital assets.
4. Taxable income above ₹50 lakh requires ITR-2 asset disclosures, including crypto holdings bought with rupees (not 'foreign assets').
5. Crypto premiums are ultra-high: the same far-OTM strikes that pay pennies on index options paid thousands of dollars.
6. Exchange P&L reports counted premium credits as profit; true P&L must be computed from wallet balance minus deposits.
7. Funds sit in a wallet and must be allocated to trading; unallocated losing positions get auto squared-off.
8. Cross-exchange USDT transfers cost $10-50 in network fees - a real drag for small accounts.
