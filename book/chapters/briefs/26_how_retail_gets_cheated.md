## Key lessons
- [2756] Regulators release combined buyer-seller loss data; the honest question is what share of losers are option buyers versus sellers - pressure on sellers shrinks supply and raises premiums, hurting buyers more.
- [2802] Ask three questions before paying: total charges, recurring charges (the 72,000/year software trap that ate half a 2-lakh account), and the trainer's own audited trading record - MCA filings cost 100 rupees.
- [2802] Scams evolve (digital arrest, romance-crypto pig butchering via matrimonial sites); the defense is always the same: verify identity live, verify custody of money, never transfer to third-party accounts.
- [2205] When a promoted platform faces allegations, promoters should verify claims (speaker had his team check whether tradex offered RBI-prohibited forex products) and be ready to end the relationship.
- [2748] Retail collateral rules cap net short exposure (500-crore rule), yet one foreign entity held ~45,000 crore of options - limits must apply at ultimate-beneficiary-owner level, not per entity.
- [2756] Bank Nifty with 12 stocks where four hold ~80% (HDFC Bank ~30%) can be moved by a 1,000-crore order in two names - index design itself enables manipulation; want 15+ stocks, max 10% each.
- [0304] A stock makes 90% of its move in 10% of the time - boredom is the price of holding (Reliance flat 2008-2017, then +400% in 3 years; a power stock flat a decade, then 3x in 3 months).
- [0304] Trainers monetize the desperate: overpriced software (Rs60,000/yr, Rs30,000 commission) and fictitious model portfolios (Rs3L to Rs40L, '2000%') target people who just lost 90%.
- [1118] Every centralized crypto exchange carries custody/counterparty risk; there is no independent depository to verify holdings, so registration jurisdiction and regulation matter.
- [2802] The math test: Indian businesses rarely clear 25-30% p.a. - anyone promising 100-200% from a weekend course is selling fantasy; even the trainer's own P&L (if filed) shows it.
