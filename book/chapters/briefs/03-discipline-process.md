## Core lessons
1. High-VIX regimes let low-volume sellers earn outsized returns as VIX collapses - the profit engine is vega plus view, not volume.
2. Spread risk across many strikes (6,500 through 8,500 puts) and multiple expiries (weekly/May/June) - never concentrate one strike.
3. Position-level losses (up to ~1.75 lakh single positions) coexist with net profit - judge the portfolio, not each leg.
4. Never copy aggressive sizing: capital and risk appetite differ; matching the trade without matching the capital is ruinous.
5. Staying flat or small is respectable - 'you must be happy as long as you are not losing money.'
6. Markets can absorb good or bad news but not uncertainty; once an outcome is known, clarity itself re-prices risk.
7. When 90% expect a fall, the fall is already priced - consensus trades usually fail.
8. Option buyers can be right on direction and still lose when uncertainty resolves and premiums collapse.

## Strategies
- **Strike-and-expiry put ladder (illustrative, high risk)**: Sold puts at 6,500 / 7,000 / 7,300 / 7,400 / 7,500 / 7,800 / 8,500 across weekly, May and June expiries; small stock-opt
- **Multi-strike, multi-expiry put selling (April 2020)**: Sold put options spread across various strike prices and expiry periods rather than one strike
- **Put-selling ladder in collapsing-VIX rebound**: Sold puts across strikes from ~6,100 to 9,000 during April 2020 recovery
- **Staggered index investing with cash-secured puts**: SIP into a Nifty 50 ETF; optionally place cash-secured puts at desired lower entries
- **Cash-secured puts (ICICI 300PE, HDFC 840PE)**: Sell puts below market with full cash set aside for delivery
