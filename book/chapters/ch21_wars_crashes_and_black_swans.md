# Chapter 21: Wars, Crashes and Black Swans

## The Day the World Changed

On February 24, 2022, I was sitting in my office in Mumbai, watching the pre-open screen. The Gift Nifty was already down 400 points. Then the news broke: Russia had invaded Ukraine. By 9:15 AM, the cash market opened and Nifty fell like a stone—900 points in the first hour. Bank Nifty dropped 2,000 points. It was the worst single-day fall since the COVID circuit breaker in March 2020. Panic selling swept every sector. Traders who had been long for weeks saw their margins evaporate. Stop-losses were triggered in cascades. By midday, the VIX had spiked to 35, and the market was down nearly 5%.

But here’s what happened next. By Friday, just two days later, global markets staged a dramatic recovery. Sanctions on Russia were announced, but they were less severe than feared. Oil prices, which had spiked to $130, began to ease. Nifty recovered 600 points that Friday. The war was still raging, but the market had already priced in the worst.

That day taught me something I have never forgotten: **threats move markets more than wars do.** The uncertainty before an event is far more damaging than the event itself. Once the uncertainty is resolved—even if the news is bad—markets begin to climb the wall of worry.

This chapter is about trading those rare, high-impact events: wars, crashes, and black swans. These are the moments that separate amateurs from professionals. The amateurs panic. The professionals prepare.

---

## What Is a Black Swan?

The term “black swan” was popularized by Nassim Taleb. It describes an event that:

- Is rare and unpredictable
- Has massive consequences
- Seems obvious in hindsight

In Indian markets, we have seen three black swans in the last 15 years: the Lehman Brothers collapse in 2008, the COVID-19 crash in March 2020, and the inflation shock of 2022. Each was unexpected. Each caused a 20–40% drawdown. Each was followed by a recovery that most traders missed because they were too scared to buy.

Wars are a special category of black swans. They are rare, but they are not entirely unpredictable. Geopolitical tensions build over weeks or months. The market often discounts the possibility before the actual event. That is why the actual invasion day—the “shooting war”—often produces a smaller reaction than the threat period.

## How Indian Markets Have Reacted to Wars

I have studied every major geopolitical event affecting India since 1999. Here is what the numbers tell us:

| Event | Date | Nifty Fall (approx.) | Recovery Time |
|-------|------|---------------------|---------------|
| Kargil War | May–July 1999 | ~7% over weeks | 2 months |
| Parliament Attack | Dec 13, 2001 | ~5% in one day | 3 weeks |
| Mumbai Attacks | Nov 26, 2008 | ~4% in two days | 1 month |
| Pulwama Attack | Feb 14, 2019 | ~6% in one week | 2 weeks |
| Russia-Ukraine Invasion | Feb 24, 2022 | ~5% in one day | 1 week |

Notice the pattern: the average fall is 4–7%. The recovery is quick—usually within a few weeks. Why? Because wars are not economic events. They are emotional events. The market sells first and asks questions later. Once the initial shock passes, traders realize that the underlying economy has not collapsed. Companies still earn profits. Banks still lend. People still consume.

The key insight: **Do not extrapolate the knee-jerk reaction.** A 5% fall on war news does not mean the market will fall 20%. It usually means the opposite—a buying opportunity for those with cash and courage.

## The Real Channel: Uncertainty, Not War

When Russia invaded Ukraine, the Gift Nifty was down only 30–40 points the night before. Compare that to the Russia-Ukraine threat period in January 2022, when Nifty fell 1,200 points over two weeks. The actual invasion was a “non-event” for India relative to the buildup.

The same pattern repeated with the Israel-Hamas war in October 2023. The Gift Nifty barely moved. Why? Because the market had already priced in the risk. India is not directly involved. The threat to Indian markets was minimal.

But when the US-China trade war escalated in 2019, Nifty fell 10% over three months. That was a real economic threat—tariffs hurt corporate earnings.

So the rule is simple: **Threats move markets more than wars do.** Trade the uncertainty, not the event.

## The Three Phases of a Black Swan

Every black swan event follows a predictable pattern. If you understand the phases, you can trade them profitably.

### Phase 1: Pre-Event (Uncertainty Builds)

This is the most dangerous phase. The market is nervous. Volatility rises. The VIX climbs. Options premiums become expensive. Everyone is guessing: will there be a war? Will the central bank hike rates? Will the scam blow up?

During this phase, the smartest thing you can do is **stay light**. Reduce your position size. Keep cash. If you must trade, use options to hedge.

I recall a trader who, in February 2022, bought a Nifty 17,000 put for ₹150 when Nifty was at 17,500. The invasion happened two weeks later. That put was worth ₹2,500 at the low. He made 16x his money. But he also lost money on his long futures. Net, he broke even. That is the point of hedging: not to make a killing, but to survive.

### Phase 2: The Event (Panic and Capitulation)

The event hits. The market gaps down. Stop-losses trigger. Margin calls go out. Retail traders sell in a frenzy. The VIX spikes to 30, 40, even 50.

This is the moment when most people freeze. They watch their portfolio bleed and do nothing. They hope for a recovery that does not come—not yet.

The professional does the opposite. He waits for **capitulation volume**. That is the moment when the selling is so intense that even the strongest stocks fall. Defensive stocks like ITC fall 6% in a day. That is a signal of broad capitulation. When everything is red, the bottom is near.

But here is the catch: **markets rarely bottom exactly at the 200-DMA.** They overshoot. Like a braking car, they skid past the stop sign before coming to a halt. In March 2020, Nifty broke below its 200-DMA and kept falling another 15% before finding a bottom. In June 2024, on election day, Nifty fell 8.5% intraday—far below its 200-DMA—and recovered only after the coalition news broke.

So do not try to catch the exact bottom. Wait for the first green close. Wait for the VIX to start falling. Then buy.

### Phase 3: Post-Event (Recovery)

Once the uncertainty is resolved, the market begins to recover. The recovery is often faster than the fall. In 2020, Nifty recovered its entire COVID loss in 6 months. In 2024, it recovered the election loss in 3 months and went on to hit record highs.

The recovery is driven by two forces:

1. **Short covering**: Traders who sold during the panic buy back to close positions.
2. **Value buying**: Long-term investors see bargains and accumulate.

The sectors that lead the recovery are usually the ones that fell the most. In 2020, it was banks and auto. In 2024, it was PSU banks and infrastructure.

## Worked Example: Trading the Russia-Ukraine Invasion

Let me walk you through a realistic trade. Assume you are a trader with ₹10 lakh capital on February 23, 2022.

### Pre-Event (Feb 14–23)

You see the news: Russia has massed troops on Ukraine’s border. The US warns of invasion. Nifty has already fallen from 18,000 to 17,200. The VIX is at 22.

You decide to hedge. You buy one lot of Nifty 17,000 Put (expiry March) for ₹150. One lot = 50 units. Cost = ₹7,500.

You also reduce your long futures position from 5 lots to 2 lots. Your exposure drops from ₹85 lakh to ₹34 lakh.

### Event Day (Feb 24)

Nifty opens at 16,800, down 400 points. By 10 AM, it hits 16,300. Your put is now worth ₹1,200. You sell it for a profit of ₹52,500 (₹1,050 per unit × 50).

But your 2 long futures lots have lost 900 points each = 2 × 50 × 900 = ₹90,000 loss.

Net loss = ₹90,000 – ₹52,500 = ₹37,500. Painful, but manageable. Without the hedge, you would have lost ₹2.25 lakh on 5 lots.

### Recovery (Feb 25–March 5)

On Friday Feb 25, Nifty recovers 600 points to 16,900. You buy back 2 more lots of futures at 16,900. By March 5, Nifty is at 17,500. Your 4 lots are now up 600 points each = 4 × 50 × 600 = ₹1.2 lakh profit.

Total P&L for the period: –₹37,500 (event day) + ₹1.2 lakh (recovery) = ₹82,500 net profit.

That is a 8.25% return on capital in two weeks. Not bad for a war.

## The Checklist for Trading Black Swans

Here is a simple checklist I use. You can adapt it to your style.

**Before the event (when uncertainty is high):**

- [ ] Reduce position size to 50% of normal.
- [ ] Buy out-of-the-money puts (10–15% below current price) as insurance.
- [ ] Keep at least 30% cash.
- [ ] Avoid sectors directly exposed to the event (e.g., oil stocks during a war, banks during a scam).

**During the event (the panic day):**

- [ ] Do not trade in the first 30 minutes. Let the volatility settle.
- [ ] Look for capitulation signals: defensive stocks falling, VIX above 35, volume 2x average.
- [ ] If you are hedged, do not add to shorts. The risk of a snap rally is high.
- [ ] If you are unhedged, do not panic sell. Wait for the close. The worst is usually over by 2 PM.

**After the event (the recovery):**

- [ ] Wait for the first green close on the Nifty.
- [ ] Buy futures or calls on the index or beaten-down sectors.
- [ ] Sell your puts once the VIX starts falling.
- [ ] Hold for at least 2–4 weeks. The recovery is rarely a one-day affair.

## The Biggest Mistake: Overreacting

The most common mistake traders make during black swans is overreacting. They see a 5% fall and assume the market is going to zero. They sell everything. Then they watch the market recover without them.

I have seen this happen in every crash. In March 2020, traders sold at 8,000 and swore they would never come back. By November, Nifty was at 13,000. They had missed a 60% rally.

The antidote is simple: **Have a plan before the event.** Write down what you will do if Nifty falls 5%, 10%, or 20%. Stick to the plan. Do not let emotions hijack your decisions.

## The Final Word

Wars, crashes, and black swans are terrifying. They test your discipline, your risk management, and your nerve. But they are also the greatest opportunities in the market. The best trades of my career have come during the worst days.

Remember: markets climb the wall of worry. When all uncertainty is resolved, it is time to sell, not buy. The time to buy is when the uncertainty is at its peak—when the news is bad, the VIX is high, and everyone is scared.

That is the secret. And now you know it.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Threats move markets more than wars do.** Trade the uncertainty buildup, not the event itself.
2. **Historical war reactions in India average 4–7% falls with quick recoveries.** Do not extrapolate the knee-jerk.
3. **Use options to hedge before the event.** A small premium can save your portfolio from a large loss.
4. **Wait for capitulation signals (defensive stocks falling, VIX spike) before buying the dip.**
5. **Have a written plan for every black swan scenario.** Stick to it. Do not let fear or greed decide your trades.
