## Insurance Is Not a Bet

Let me say this plainly: hedging is not a prediction. It is not a view that the market will fall. It is insurance, just like the policy you buy for your house or your car.

When you buy fire insurance for your home, you do not expect your house to burn down. You pay the premium because the one time it does, you want to be protected. Hedging works the same way.

The mistake most Indian investors make is treating hedging as a trade. They buy a put option thinking the market will fall. When it doesn't, they feel cheated. They stop buying protection. And then the crash comes.

A hedge is insurance. It compensates but does not remove the loss. A 300-point hedge locks that loss when the market falls—meaning you still lose 300 points, but you do not lose 1,000.

That distinction matters.

## The Core Problem: Markets Are Not Always Rational

I have been in this market for over two decades. I can tell you with certainty that markets do things that make no mathematical sense—until they do.

Consider June 4, 2024. The exit polls had predicted a comfortable majority for the ruling coalition. The market had priced that in. When the actual counting showed a weaker mandate, the Nifty fell 8.5% intraday. That night, two regional parties pledged support, and the government was formed. The index eventually recovered and went on to hit record highs 32 times in the remainder of the year.

But here is the question: could you have predicted the exact sequence of that day? No. Could you have known that the recovery would happen within hours, not days? No.

What you could have done was decide in advance: "If my portfolio drops more than X% in a single day, I want a floor under it."

That is what hedging does.

## The Hedging Toolkit for Indian Investors

The Indian derivatives market offers several tools. Let me walk you through the ones that actually work for a long-term portfolio.

### 1. Index Put Options

This is the most straightforward hedge. You buy a put option on the Nifty 50 or Bank Nifty. A put option gives you the right to sell the index at a predetermined price, regardless of where the market trades.

**How it works:** If you own a ₹1 crore portfolio that broadly mirrors the Nifty, buying a 3-month Nifty put with a strike price 5% below current levels will cost you roughly 1-3% of your portfolio value. If the market falls 10%, your put option gains value by roughly the same percentage that your portfolio loses—offsetting most of the damage.

**The cost problem:** Many investors look at the premium—say, ₹30,000 for a three-month put on a ₹1 crore portfolio—and think it is too expensive. Over a year, that adds up to about ₹1.2 lakh, or 1.2% of your portfolio. If the market goes up 12% in that year, your return after hedging cost is 10.8%. That feels painful.

Until the market falls 20%.

Then your unhedged portfolio is down ₹20 lakh. Your hedged portfolio is down perhaps ₹5 lakh. The ₹1.2 lakh you spent saved you ₹15 lakh.

That is the math of insurance.

### 2. Collar Strategy (Financed Hedge)

A better solution for long-term investors is the collar. You sell a call option above the market and use that premium to buy a put option below the market.

**Worked example:**

Let us say Nifty is at 24,000. You own a portfolio worth ₹1 crore.

- **Step 1:** Buy a 23,000 put (roughly 4% below current). Premium cost: ₹1.2 lakh.
- **Step 2:** Sell a 25,200 call (roughly 5% above current). Premium received: ₹0.9 lakh.
- **Net cost:** ₹30,000 for three months, or 0.3% of your portfolio.

**What this does:**
- If Nifty falls below 23,000, the put protects you. Your maximum loss is capped at roughly 4% (plus the small net premium).
- If Nifty rises above 25,200, the call you sold limits your upside to about 5%. You miss the rally beyond that point.
- If Nifty stays between 23,000 and 25,200, you keep all the market movement minus the small net premium.

**Who should use this:** Investors who are comfortable capping their upside in exchange for capping their downside. This is ideal when valuations are high and you want protection without paying full premium.

### 3. The 3x Rule: Hedge Size for Traders

For active traders, hedging is more nuanced. A 300-point intraday drop is common in the Nifty. A 1,000-point gap-down is rare but happens.

A source I studied shows this logic clearly: hedging shrinks your margin roughly three times. If you are trading a ₹30 lakh book, hedging might reduce your available margin to ₹10 lakh. To maintain the same position size, you would need to scale up your capital three times.

That sounds like a problem. But consider this: if a ₹30 lakh unhedged book takes a ₹5 lakh loss in a crash, you are down 16%. If you had hedged with triple the capital—₹90 lakh—your loss in the same crash might be just ₹1-2 lakh, or about 2% of your total capital.

The point is not the margin. The point is the survival.

## Real Events, Real Protection

Let me show you how the June 4 event plays out with and without a hedge.

**Scenario:** You own a ₹1 crore portfolio that tracks the Nifty. On June 3 close, the Nifty is at 23,300.

**Unhedged portfolio:**
- June 4 intraday low: Nifty at 21,281 (down 8.5%)
- Your portfolio value at low: ₹91.5 lakh
- Loss at worst point: ₹8.5 lakh
- Portfolio close on June 4: Nifty at ~21,900 (down 6%)
- Loss at close: ₹6 lakh

**Hedged portfolio (using a 22,000 put bought 2 weeks earlier at a cost of ₹40,000):**
- Put option value at Nifty 21,281: The put is in-the-money by 719 points. Each point is worth ₹50 (since one Nifty lot is 50 units). So one lot of the put is worth roughly ₹35,950. If you bought 20 lots (covering your ₹1 crore exposure approximately), the put gain is about ₹7.19 lakh.
- Portfolio loss at low: ₹8.5 lakh
- Minus put gain: ₹7.19 lakh
- Net loss at worst point: ₹1.31 lakh
- Plus the ₹40,000 premium you already paid: total cost ₹1.71 lakh

Your maximum loss on the worst day of the year was 1.7% of your portfolio. Everyone else lost 6-8.5%.

That is what protection looks like.

## When NOT to Hedge

Hedging is not always the answer. Let me give you three situations where you should skip it.

**1. Long-term SIP investors with a 10+ year horizon**
If you are systematically investing every month into an index fund and plan to hold for decades, hedging does not make sense. Your time horizon is your hedge. Market crashes are buying opportunities.

**2. When the cost is absurdly high**
During extreme fear events, put option premiums can spike to 5-8% of portfolio value. If implied volatility is through the roof, wait for it to settle. Alternatively, use a collar to finance the cost.

**3. When you are fully in cash**
If you have already exited the market, you do not need a hedge. Your cash is your protection.

## The Hidden Enemy: Volatility Decay

I learned this lesson the hard way early in my career. I bought a put option in August 2018. The market was overbought, the news was negative, and I was certain a fall was coming.

The market did not fall. It went sideways for three weeks. Each day, the option lost a little time value. By the end of the month, my put had lost 70% of its value—even though the market hadn't moved.

Then the market fell. But my option was now too small to help.

This is called volatility decay—specifically, theta decay. Options lose value every day they remain open, even if the market does nothing. To hedge effectively, you need to:

- Buy protection when volatility is low (premiums are cheap)
- Roll the hedge periodically (sell the near-term put, buy a longer-dated one)
- Accept that sometimes your hedge will expire worthless

The last point is the hardest for most people. They see the expired option as a loss. It is not. It is a premium paid for protection that you did not need to use—just like your car insurance premium is not a "loss" when you don't have an accident.

## A Practical Hedging Framework

Here is a simple system that works for most portfolios:

**Step 1: Determine your risk budget**
Decide how much of your annual return you are willing to spend on hedging. I recommend 1-3% of portfolio value per year. If your portfolio is ₹1 crore, budget ₹1-3 lakh annually.

**Step 2: Choose your time frame**
Never buy one-week options for portfolio hedging. The theta decay is too fast. Buy options with at least 3 months to expiry. Roll them every 60 days to avoid the rapid decay of the final month.

**Step 3: Set your strike**
For a portfolio hedge, buy puts at 5-8% below the current market. This keeps the premium affordable while covering the kind of drop that would actually hurt.

**Step 4: Execute systematically**
Pick a date each quarter—say the first Monday of January, April, July, and October—and roll your hedge. Do not time it. Do not try to guess. Just do it.

**Step 5: Never remove the hedge during fear**
This is the most important rule. When the market is crashing, your hedge is working. Do not sell it to "lock in profits." Let it run. The worst thing you can do is close your put at the bottom of a crash—that defeats the entire purpose.

## The Math of Staying in the Game

I have seen too many talented investors blow up because they refused to hedge. They believed in their analysis. They believed the market would turn. They were right eventually—but they ran out of money before the turn came.

A 30% drawdown requires a 43% gain to get back to even. A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain. The deeper you fall, the harder the climb back.

Hedging does not make you rich. It keeps you alive long enough to get rich from your other strategies.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Hedging is insurance, not a trade.** Treat it like fire insurance. Pay the premium, hope you never need to use it, and be grateful when it protects you from disaster.

2. **Use index puts for portfolio protection.** The Nifty 50 options market is deep enough even for large portfolios. A 5-8% out-of-the-money put with 3-month expiry costs roughly 1-3% of portfolio value.

3. **The collar strategy reduces costs significantly.** Selling a call 5% above the market can finance most of the put premium, bringing net cost down to 0.3-0.5% of portfolio value.

4. **Hedge before the event, not after.** The time to buy protection is when the market is calm and premiums are cheap. Waiting for the VIX to spike means paying double or triple the price.

5. **Accept that some hedges will expire worthless.** That is not a mistake. That is the price of staying in the game long enough to compound your wealth.
