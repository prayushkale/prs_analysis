A mutual fund is a type of financial vehicle made up of a pool of money collected from many investors to invest in securities like stocks, bonds, money market instruments and other assets. There are many types of mutual funds. One is equity mutual funds. Other one is debt mutual funds. Even in equity mutual funds, there are large cap mutual funds, mid cap mutual funds, small cap mutual funds, sectoral mutual funds and the funds will have to invest at least 60% of their corpus in equity. Then only it will be considered as equity mutual fund. If not, it will be considered as a debt mutual fund and debt mutual funds usually invest all the money in debt instruments. What are the advantages and disadvantages of investing through mutual funds? The first one is averaging. Let us say you have got some money, you are investing in some shares and the next day or two, three days time if the price falls by 10%, 20%. Say for example, yesterday, most of the frontline stocks fell more than 10%. If you had invested day before yesterday, the yesterday would have lost 10%. But what happens in mutual funds? The people keep giving money to mutual funds on a day to day basis. So, every day they receive money. So, every day they invest. So, their prices keep averaging out. So, this is called averaging. Second one is diversifying. As a retail investor, especially in the age group of 25 to 35, your investment amount may not be very big. Mutual funds collect huge sum of money from the investors and they invest 100, 200, 300 stocks. That gives a good diversification. Let us say there is one company, some scam broke out and the price falls by 90%.
